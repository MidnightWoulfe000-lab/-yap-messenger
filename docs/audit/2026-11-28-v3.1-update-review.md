# Review of the v3.1 update

**Reviewer:** Codex, at the project owner's request.  
**Date:** 28 November 2026.  
**Revision:** HEAD `5c17f81c062d07f764bf994009ea9c210b3a4cc5`, with the working-tree documents as read. The proposals are not implemented. The input fingerprints and verification limits are recorded below.

**Verdict:** eight High and nine Medium findings; no Critical finding. The revised header, removal of `mine` and `DELIVERED`, confidential pairing proposal, and explicit batch container answer substantial parts of the previous reviews. The combined design still has blockers: inbox-key recovery can expire, clock correction cannot reliably obtain its own evidence, concurrent offers promise incompatible allocations, an empty `WANT` both settles and retries an offer, pairing completion is inconsistent with bootstrap confinement, and direct attachment encryption is incomplete. Storage transactions are a sound direction, but the quotas and incremental group-state proposal do not yet establish the promised availability or correctness. The simulator reproduces the selected stored results and corrects its earlier hop, expiry and arithmetic errors. It does not validate the wire protocol, resource limits or optimal copy counts. Keep the implementation hold; nothing here is approved for building.

**This is an AI-assisted review, not a professional security audit.** Source inspection and model checks are distinguished from tests of implemented behaviour. No independent expert review or physical-radio validation is claimed.

## Findings

### 1. High — inbox-key expiry can permanently close the recovery route

**Locations:** [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md) §§3.6–3.7, 3.11; [labels](../proposals/rotating-address-labels.md) §7; mesh v3.1 §1.5.

Suppose R changes its own inbox key from I0 to I1, sending I1 in hand-overs addressed to each recipient's existing inbox. S misses R's hand-over and still addresses pieces to I0. R deletes I0 after seven days even though S has not demonstrated use of I1; eight intervening changes can delete it sooner. S's later resynchronisation or acknowledgement cannot reach R. If both changed inboxes during a partition and lost those hand-overs, both recovery directions can fail. Keeping only each sender's newest pending hand-over can make that latest hand-over itself use a recipient inbox that has since expired. Re-wrapping its hour does not obtain a missing inbox key.

The newcomer case works only after receiving the sponsor's log, with a still-usable join inbox and appropriate prekey material. It does not establish indefinite recovery for existing members. A peer missing monthly prekey replacement can additionally construct a first message for a private prekey already deleted after 24 hours; preserving its inner bytes cannot repair that.

**Suggested fix:** specify authenticated inbox versions, activation, monotonic replacement, recipient-key selection and a recovery path that remains usable after both sides miss rotations. Explicitly choose between a bounded supported disconnection period with visible rejoining, and a separately reviewed recovery mechanism. Do not claim “always finds” with unconditional seven-day/eight-key deletion. Include two-sided partitions longer than seven days, eight rapid justified rotations, expired prekeys, rejoining and lost install ACKs. No static identity-DH fallback is authorised by this finding.

### 2. High — the clock rule cannot reliably obtain its evidence and cannot undo harmful jumps

**Locations:** [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md) §8; labels §§1, 4–5; mesh v3.1 “Also in this update”. Code: `endpoint/src/engine.rs::receive()`, `EndpointRepository.now()`, `core/src/relay.rs::normalize_clock()`.

The normal receive path rejects creation times more than five minutes ahead, yet rule 2 requires authenticated messages created more than five minutes ahead. With rotating labels, a phone hours or weeks behind may not even recognise the future label or hold/select its week's secret. The proposal supplies no bounded alternative path to authenticate that clock evidence. It also fails for a new user with fewer than two paired contacts.

Two colluding contacts can authenticate arbitrarily false creation times; two signatures establish agreement, not correct time. With a permissive evidence path they could force expiry and irreversible secret deletion. One malicious contact combined with one honest but badly set clock can advance to the earlier bad time. A single honest-clock contact limits that particular pair's jump, but no independence, freshness, maximum jump or replay rule is stated. Separately, `max(phone clock, highest time)` accepts an arbitrary local forward jump without any friend evidence and cannot recover when the user corrects it backwards.

Rule 1 also omits elapsed-time progress: after a backwards wall-clock change, literally using the saved maximum can freeze expiry until wall time catches up. Today's repository and carrier explicitly advance using monotonic elapsed time. Strangers cannot change time under rule 3, but five unauthenticated links are readily manufactured and can cause a misleading warning.

**Suggested fix:** specify the effective clock, monotonic progress, bounded evidence authentication and freshness, supported skew and a user recovery procedure before destructive pruning or stepping. Separate a suspected-time warning from permission to erase keys. Bound and confirm exceptional forward corrections rather than trusting two contacts without qualification. Avoid transmitting each phone's precise saved clock offset as a fingerprint; an optional coarse query still needs rate limits and an explicit metadata trade-off. Apply one policy to endpoint, carrier, pairing, groups and attachments, not just `meta`.

### 3. High — concurrent offers promise allocations that cannot all be reserved

**Locations:** [mesh v3.1](../proposals/local-mesh-v3.1-group-envelopes.md) §1.4, states and “Reserving copies”.

Reservations happen on transition to Sending, after `WANT`. Before that, twelve simultaneous offers from a 96-copy holder can each advertise 48. The first positive `WANT` reserves 48; the next split should be 24 from the remaining allowance. Sending 24 violates the rule that data match the offer; sending 48 violates the specified halving rule; sending all twelve advertised allocations is impossible. Atomic reservation prevents overspending only if failure and stale-offer handling are defined. It does not make the previously sent promises correct.

Spending uncertain reservations is conservative: copies can be lost after a failed write or dropped link even if the peer received nothing. That is an accepted availability cost, not a duplication bug. A peer that repeatedly wants and drops valid allocations can deliberately consume spray opportunities; spending uncertain transfers does not prove custody. Parallel arrivals can also spend two senders' allocations while the receiver retains only one, as the proposal already acknowledges.

**Suggested fix:** define when an allocation becomes fixed and what happens when an offer is stale. Options to review include reservation before the promise or invalidating and issuing a fresh offer after recomputation. Bind durable transfer identity and receiver acceptance to one allocation; define duplicate WANT/data/ACK, out-of-order replies, eviction and crash recovery. Bound outstanding reservations and retire spent reservation records. Test asynchronous replies to many offers, not only serial transfers.

### 4. High — an overload `WANT` permanently settles the very entries it should retry

**Locations:** mesh v3.1 §1.4, Offered transition, Settled state and “Budget”.

An empty `WANT` means every entry is unwanted. The state table sends unwanted entries to Settled, which is never offered again on that link. The budget paragraph instead requires an empty `WANT` above 600 entries per minute and says the offer is retried later. A busy honest link can therefore settle unseen mail permanently; hostile links can exhaust the shared budget first. This is not resolved by the no-WANT timeout, because a WANT did arrive.

**Suggested fix:** give temporary refusal a distinct bounded response/state and retry time, or explicitly define another unambiguous mechanism. Preserve permanent possession refusal separately. Test overload across several links, cap-window boundaries and a long-lived link returning to normal.

### 5. High — compact settled keys discard the digest that distinguishes envelopes

**Locations:** mesh v3.1 §1.4 “Paging”; `core/src/envelope.rs::relay_key()`; Kotlin `SecureModel.envelopeKey()`.

The first 16 bytes of a 48-byte relay key are the sender-controlled message ID, not a digest prefix. Settling by those bytes conflates two envelopes with the same ID and different immutable bytes. An attacker can copy a genuine ID into a different stamped envelope; accidental cross-sender ID collisions are unlikely, but deliberate reuse is straightforward. If the conflicting entry settles first, the real envelope is skipped on that link. This defeats the code's deliberate immutable-digest defence against poisoning an ID-only cache.

**Suggested fix:** retain the full relay key, or use a reviewed compact identifier derived from the complete key with appropriate collision handling. Do not truncate to its ID field. Test equal IDs with different destinations, ciphertext, class and creation times.

### 6. High — pairing has no single completion and cancellation boundary

**Locations:** [encrypted pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md) §§2–4; labels §§1–2; [endpoint contract](../android-endpoint-v1.md), bootstrap phases; `engine.rs::confirm()` and receive kinds 3–4; `SecureModel.requestBundle()`, `localRecord()` and `pump()`.

The proposal says a contact is ready when both confirmations validate “as today”. Today mutual confirmation starts bootstrap: the responder becomes ready after accepting kind 03 and the initiator after its authenticated kind-04 acknowledgement. The new text cancels a dropped link only before confirmation completion, yet requires the first libsignal message to stay inside that one Noise channel. If the link drops after confirmations but before bootstrap/its ACK, the text neither cancels nor supplies an allowed retry route. “Today's bootstrap stays unchanged” retains a durable normal-outbox retry path that conflicts with strict pairing-queue confinement. The local phone cannot simply know both peers have committed the final step.

Discovery is also not “by the destination token, as today”: today's hub HELLO is rotating ID plus nonce; pairing sends a 32-byte bundle commitment in `BUNDLE_REQUEST` to all local links. A fresh-token discovery protocol could be specified, but it is not the existing mechanism. Noise handshake/transport frame types and admission, including simultaneous attempts and restart cancellation, are not defined. Old BUNDLE and CONFIRM handlers must not remain a cleartext fallback. The optional pairing-file warning does not establish the pair secret or satisfy the new channel contract.

**Suggested fix:** define exact local states through bootstrap and key installation, the readiness boundary, cancellation/cleanup at every step and a recovery rule after either side commits. A cancelled attempt must not later publish an old outbox packet. Specify discovery, new frame encodings and queue routing before content dispatch. For this update, remove the file route unless a complete confidential file exchange is separately reviewed; a warning alone does not integrate it.

### 7. High — direct attachment encryption is not a complete authenticated channel

**Locations:** [attachments](../proposals/attachments-v1.md) §3; mesh v3.1 §§3.1, 3.3–3.4, 3.9.

“HKDF from linkKey and both nonces” does not define the cipher, nonce allocation, directional keys, canonical nonce order, authenticated frame fields, replay rules, counters, retransmission or restart behaviour. Both endpoints could use the same key and overlapping frame nonces. A week boundary can give them different `linkKey`s even when recognition succeeds using an older key. PROBE recognition names a shared secret; it is not mutual key confirmation for this transfer protocol.

Attachments now use recognition for routing and change observable traffic after a match, contrary to Part 3's display-only and no-wire-change rules. Caps exhausted by strangers can therefore block big attachments, not just remove screen names. Hiding is asymmetric: A can hide its tag but still recognise B. A literal “offer to a recognised contact” implementation on A would still offer the attachment unless it also checks A's local switch and the protocol establishes the intended reciprocal permission. The claim that hiding is invisible also needs qualification when attachment offers/progress are observable. A transparent tunnel remains able to carry the transfer; “both nearby” is not proved.

**Suggested fix:** use a separately specified standard authenticated channel, such as an appropriate Noise handshake, or provide the complete reviewed AEAD record contract and key-confirmation requirements. Bind identities, roles and this link's transcript; define a common key epoch and restart-safe resume. Make mutual hiding/cap behaviour explicit and revise the display-only/privacy wording. Do not use recognition as evidence of distance.

### 8. High — storage and attachment limits do not reserve capacity for other work

**Locations:** local storage §§3–5, 7; attachments §§2–6; mesh v3.1 §§1.3–1.4.

“Expires in 24 hours” bounds age, not bytes, row count or work. Outbox and received rows have no count cap; outbound creation has no stated rate; the 1,000/hour limit is per contact or group sender, not global. The fair-share rule refuses large chats only near capacity and does not evict existing history or reserve room for receipts, key changes, identities and other chats. Once everyone has filled their share, “every other chat keeps working” cannot hold. Rejected data must not prevent state-reducing receipts or safety-critical group updates.

The group and carrier add pending pieces, retained keys, last-copy seen records, reservation records and label tables. Their counts, bytes, retention and admission paths are not covered by the history limit. A last-copy drop still creates a 24-hour record, even though it stores no envelope. Per-link settled state sized for the current 10,000-envelope store is not automatically enough for all offers encountered as that store turns over.

For attachments, “20 big transfers per contact” allows 2 GB per contact before overhead and says nothing about incoming orphan pieces, incomplete files, concurrent transfers, unsolicited chunks or all contacts together. The 5 GB setting is not an admission/reservation algorithm. A sender can announce a plausible file and never finish it, or keep changing IDs. Limits must apply before allocating, writing or invoking decoders, including thumbnails and compressed media.

**Suggested fix:** specify global and per-chat byte/work limits, reserved control capacity, persistent size accounting and safe low-space behaviour. Reserve attachment space before acceptance, bound orphan/incomplete state and active streams, enforce chunk ranges and declared totals before writes, and define cleanup after expiry/crash. Account for physical database growth, temporary files and compaction space as well as logical values. Audit finding 5 is not closed merely by replacing a count limit with disk exhaustion.

### 9. Medium — incremental group state needs a fallback for a newly arriving branch

**Locations:** local storage §6; `groups/src/state.rs::Log::insert()`, `derive()`, `overruled()` and `apply()`.

The code does cache validity, depth, active count and SuperAdmin status on insertion, so that claim is right. However, insertion derives the state of the event's ancestors, and deriving the result recomputes concurrent-decision overrides before applying the ordered events. An arriving event whose parent is an old ancestor can introduce a second branch without being a merge event. For example, a newly received SuperAdmin decision concurrent with an already applied ordinary-admin decision can override that earlier decision. Applying the new event alone to the materialised roster cannot necessarily restore all consequences of the earlier event.

The proposed fast path for a genuinely linear extension of the complete current heads is plausible: the new event follows the existing history, so it cannot newly create that concurrency. But “only an event that joins two branches” triggers replay in §6; it must also cover an arriving branch, not just a merge. There is no public incremental API yet. The 100 ms target is a measurement goal, not evidence that the refactoring preserves governance.

**Suggested fix:** retain full derivation as the reference and fall back for any insertion that does not satisfy the proven complete-heads fast-path precondition. Specify the cached state and branch/override recalculation. Differentially compare with replay across forks, late arrivals, merges, invalid events, removals, settings and elections. Optimise the genuinely linear case only when equivalent; benchmark after correctness is established.

### 10. Medium — the new binding is coherent, but the surrounding wire sizes and type are not

**Locations:** mesh v3.1 §§1.1, 1.4; labels §5; group encryption §3.4; `core/src/envelope.rs`; `MeshHub.kt::MeshFrames` and `Peer.write()`.

The offset sum is correct: 4 + 1 + 1 + 16 + 16 + 16 + 8 + 8 = 70. Hops are byte 70; preserving today's four-byte ciphertext length makes it bytes 71–74 and makes the new envelope header 75 bytes. The proposal should say this explicitly and update `core::relay::peek_stored()` as well as Kotlin's offsets. Relay keys should zero only the new hop byte and retain the rest of the encoding.

The text calls `ENVELOPE` type 1, which is HELLO in today's code; ENVELOPE is 4. No intentional reassignment is defined. An encoded 65,536-byte envelope plus new offer ID (8), index (1), allocation (1), stamp (8) and type (1) needs 65,555 frame bytes, exceeding `MAX_FRAME = 65,537`. Even today's nine-byte prefix has a full-envelope edge mismatch. A 64-entry OFFER is correctly 3,209 payload bytes, or 3,210 including type; it fits. The proposed PROBE also fits.

**Suggested fix:** publish the complete versioned envelope and frame table, choose the actual maximum envelope payload or enlarged frame cap, and change all decoder/size/native bounds together. Include a maximum-size envelope test. The binding change itself does not need another primitive; it needs the new vectors already promised.

### 11. Medium — equal ACKs leave active tracking and batch-label retention oracles

**Locations:** mesh v3.1 §§1.3–1.5; group encryption §3.11; labels' privacy goals.

WANT is a possession/seen-set oracle even without destination labels. Querying an observed relay key needs no stamp. Planting a marker requires a valid stamped envelope, but the same captured envelope and stamp can be reused at different times and targets; a fresh stamp is not required for each query or target. The 600-entry budget limits rate, not this capability. Recognising a phone across a link-ID change is explicitly possible.

A member keeping and re-offering a received last copy reveals a relationship to that envelope. Waiting for an ID rotation does not fix an attacker deliberately looking for that same envelope under the new ID; it can help link the IDs. Moreover, §3.11 retains a batch on a matching visible piece label even if its piece fails decryption. Someone can copy an observed label into a new, correctly stamped but otherwise invalid batch and test later retention/re-offering. That establishes label recognition, not authenticated membership. Existing members know inbox keys, so outer decryption also cannot authenticate a particular sender against other members; inner peer and membership checks remain mandatory.

The ACK time includes label lookup and store/drop work. Those paths differ and a batch may require up to 128 lookups. Identical bytes at the same conceptual stage do not establish indistinguishable timing. §3.11 also says every mesh ACK means stored, contradicting the ACK deliberately sent after dropping a last copy. A sender must never treat that ACK as proof of durable custody.

**Suggested fix:** state these residual capabilities accurately and distinguish accepted metadata exposure from properties still claimed. Correct the stamp statement and ACK meaning. Review whether retention should require authenticated piece acceptance, with bounded work, rather than a copied label alone. Measure store/drop timing if indistinguishability is required. No claim is made that an equal-ACK implementation already leaks measured timing; none exists to measure.

### 12. Medium — padding HMAC counts does not pad the complete recognition operation

**Locations:** mesh v3.1 §§3.2–3.4 and tests.

128 generation HMACs and 256 × 128 constant-time comparisons are fixed bounds. The text does not pad contact loading, HKDF derivation, current/previous-week lookup, missing-key handling, random throwaway-key generation, collision bookkeeping or result selection. Random-key work occurs for unused places; real-key work can differ. Equal HMAC counts therefore do not prove that timing is independent of contact count or matches. Week catch-up can add still more variable work.

The separate pre-native caps and four waiting slots answer the earlier queue issue in principle. They still need an exact rolling-window/burst policy, cancellation rules and cheap overload path, including generation of filler probes. A stranger can exhaust them and observe the resulting degradation; attachment dependence makes it functional as well as visual.

**Suggested fix:** specify what whole-operation timing property is required, cache/precompute or pad all relevant work consistently, bound catch-up separately and measure complete observable exchanges at 0, 1 and 128 contacts with/without a match. Keep “second PROBE leaves first result” as written; it resolves the earlier ambiguous state change.

### 13. Medium — weekly secrets need bounded catch-up and symmetric week-boundary matching

**Locations:** labels §1; mesh v3.1 §§3.3–3.4; attachments §3.

The HKDF step is a reasonable one-way deterministic evolution with purpose-separated derivations. A copy of week W computes all future weeks; erasure only protects weeks older than every retained secret. The proposal correctly gives up post-compromise recovery and requires removal/re-pairing. A dormant phone can step repeatedly from its saved week without receiving anything, provided it has the original week index and a credible target time. The number of steps, its resource bound and atomic deletion/checkpoint rules are not stated.

Pairing also needs one agreed initial week index. The secret is described as belonging to “the week pairing happened in”, but the signed confirmations cover the secret rather than an explicit common starting week. Phones pairing on opposite sides of a week boundary can assign that same secret to different week numbers and derive different successors permanently. Bind the initial root/week identifier into the confirmed pairing transcript and persist it with the contact; deterministic stepping only agrees when its initial indexed state agrees.

At a boundary, the advanced receiver checking current and previous recognises the slower sender. The slower receiver checking its current and previous cannot recognise the advanced sender's next-week tag. This is asymmetric despite the claimed two-sided boundary test. Deriving next week “when needed” in the labels proposal does not override the explicit recognition list. A contact paired this week has no previous-week root at all; this needs a dummy path rather than a backwards derivation. Future label-table entries can require next week's root near the boundary too.

**Suggested fix:** define exactly which bounded weeks each operation generates/checks, handle absent pre-pairing weeks, and reconcile this with the fixed probe-work budget. Preserve the overlap needed for 24-hour mail and refuse creation times before this contact's pairing generation. Test both clock orderings, a mid-week new contact, weeks offline, restoration of an old copy and a crash during catch-up. Restored copies still face the separately open rollback problem; deterministic stepping does not make two restored libsignal sessions safe.

### 14. Medium — the storage inventory and filesystem transaction boundary are incomplete

**Locations:** local storage §§2–4, 7–9; group encryption §§3.2, 3.6, 3.11 and §5; attachments §4.

The table list covers contact pair-secret state but describes sessions only per contact. Group-only sessions must not become contacts: it needs group/member/session-generation addressing, last-resort prekey-use records, accepted-inner-piece digests and exact ACKs, pending hand-overs, deferred generations, old/current inbox keys and versions, group replay records and last-copy seen state. “Hand-over state” is not yet a storage/retention contract. Pairing says restart cancels an attempt while the table persists its keys; that lifecycle must be reconciled explicitly.

Database transactions cannot atomically delete attachment files or create/replace the Android wrapped store-key file and Keystore entry. §7 promises contact deletion including attachments “in one transaction”, but files live outside the database. A committed reference to a file that was never durably published loses data; deleting a file before committing reference removal can break rollback. Missing wrappers with an existing database correctly fail closed, but interrupted first creation and leftover wrapper/alias combinations need defined recovery without silently overwriting an identity.

Moving from snapshot AES-GCM to record AEAD also permits record deletion or restoration of an older valid value at its original key/schema: AD prevents relocation, not freshness or completeness. That remains an open rollback threat, not an improvement established by record encryption. Compaction is not a guarantee of secret erasure from old encrypted pages under a still-live store key. Initial inbox keys in signed full governance logs remain retained historical material; the old-inbox erasure claim must distinguish those copies.

**Suggested fix:** publish a complete record/key/retention inventory, including group-only state and the carrier's independent clock. Specify durable file publication, deletion tombstones/cleanup and restart reconciliation, keeping network/UI publication after the relevant commits. State the record-replay and flash-remanence limits. Include carrier old-format cleanup in the coordinated upgrade; deleting only the old endpoint snapshot does not empty `relay-v1.redb`.

### 15. Medium — STREAM and attachment reassembly lack an exact authenticated format

**Locations:** attachments §§1–2, 5–6; mesh frame limits in finding 10.

64 KiB plaintext chunks are a reasonable starting size, but “STREAM over XChaCha” leaves the STREAM variant, initial stream nonce, version, final-segment marker and serialization unspecified. The announcement authenticates hash and count only if acceptance checks the exact announced ciphertext, byte length, segment count and final segment. Per-segment AEAD alone cannot tell an application that stopped reading early that a final segment is missing. A random file key per attachment is helpful but does not supply the missing nonce/format rules.

Mesh pieces are up to 60 KiB and are not the same boundaries as the 64 KiB STREAM segments; the receiver must reassemble the canonical encrypted byte stream before segment decoding. Direct FILE_CHUNK cannot blindly hold a 64 KiB segment plus its tag and headers under the current frame cap. The piece format also needs sender/group scope, total bounds, duplicate/conflicting-piece handling and a manifest association before reserving storage. Outer authentication is shared among group members and does not by itself supply the sender authentication of a normal group message.

**Suggested fix:** select the library's standard STREAM variant and document its exact nonce/segment format. Bind a canonical attachment manifest to the authenticated announcing sender and check all totals and the final segment before exposing the file. Define bounded reassembly and transport fragmentation independently. Retain random file keys and standard library primitives; do not build a new streaming cipher.

### 16. Medium — the new inbox field and batch layout invalidate the retained size evidence

**Locations:** group encryption §§3.9, 3.11, §5 “Encodings”; `experiments/sender-key-sizes/src/main.rs::hand_over()` and `PIECE_OVERHEAD`.

§3.11 distributes a new inbox key inside every key hand-over, but the canonical `YGH1` list and size experiment contain no inbox-key field or version. The measured hand-over cannot install that key as specified. The experiment also still counts a two-byte batch length prefix: its overhead is 70 bytes. `YGB1` now uses a four-byte length, making label/hour/wrapper/length overhead 72 bytes per piece, plus the five-byte container prefix. Adding the inbox key also changes encrypted sizes and can change batching boundaries. Thus the retained 387/419/899 and 2,036/2,068/2,548-byte measurements are for the preceding hand-over, not this whole revision.

The new container's maximum arithmetic itself fits: 5 + 28 × 128 + 56 × 1,024 = 60,933 bytes, before the envelope header. Bounds on the sum and exact remaining length are sound if checked before allocation with checked arithmetic. A bounded 128-label scan does not bound construction of the lookup table: up to eight retained inbox keys multiplies 27 × 127 by eight, before multiple groups/collisions. Key changes must update the table immediately, not only membership changes/hourly rebuilds.

**Suggested fix:** complete the canonical hand-over and inbox-version encoding, update the measurement and vectors, and refresh costs from actual bytes. Define collision candidates, retained-key table bounds, per-group key scope and cache invalidation. Require minimum wrapper length and supported hours before opening pieces; malformed aggregate lengths reject the whole batch, while an individually invalid piece remains isolated as intended.

### 17. Medium — simulator conclusions overstate equivalence and the storage maximum

**Locations:** simulator [FINDINGS](../../experiments/copy-count-sim/FINDINGS.md) §§1–3, [raw results](../../experiments/copy-count-sim/results/results.csv), `CopyCountSim.java::rows()` and `ms()`; mesh v3.1 §1.3; second-response simulator summary.

The text claims the same recipient shares and delays in every scenario, but its own table has 85% versus 81% all-member delivery for group 30 in `scattered`. The raw five-seed means are 84.668% and 81.334%; mean medians are 6.7026 and 6.6736 hours and mean p90s 15.3896 and 15.4508. These are close descriptive outcomes, not exact equality, proof of equivalence or a general guarantee that privacy has no delivery cost. Five seeds and three fixed groups per size per seed do not establish statistical equivalence.

The crowd traffic figures are supported as rounded means: one-to-one 849.46 versus 94.612 sends, and group 30 980.346 versus 279.912. The crowd P “busiest phone” summary averages each seed's peak: 208.4. The actual maximum is 228. ASK's mean peak is 135.4 and maximum 150, not a measured maximum of about 160. ALL's mean peak is 898.8 and maximum 900. Neither counts bytes or enforces a storage cap.

**Suggested fix:** report similar model delivery with the observed differences, separate means from maxima, and retain reached-only latency denominators. Say that measured occupancy is small at this artificial load; the model does not demonstrate bounded byte storage, eviction safety, radio capacity, battery cost or real-world equivalence. Neither 96 nor 255 is established as optimal.

There is also a remaining wire/model mismatch in `Run.offer()`: `newHops == 0 && !endpoint` returns before charging a transmission. A holder with one hop left and one copy therefore sends to an unseen member but does not send to an unseen stranger. Under the private last-copy rule it cannot know which peer is a member before sending; the stranger still receives the bytes before dropping them. A focused harness against the unchanged source returned `sent=0, transmissions=0` for the stranger and `sent=1, transmissions=1, stored hops=0` for the member. Zero-hop carrier refusal is correct, but eliminating the preceding radio transmission uses the very recipient oracle the new rule removes. Correct that decision and rerun traffic and delivery comparisons; the budgets it saves can affect other messages too.

## First-review answers, in the requested order

### Encrypted pairing

1. **Noise pattern.** `NNpsk0` is a reasonable candidate with a fresh high-entropy QR-derived PSK and identity signatures verified inside the channel. It avoids a second static identity-key interface. `XXpsk3` is not automatically better for the stated threat model and would add static-key ownership/binding work; do not silently reuse libsignal private keys as Noise static keys. Require fresh ephemeral keys, explicit user confirmation, attempt expiry and complete tests. The first Noise message still exposes its ephemeral public key and framing; “everything encrypted from the first byte” should mean confidential pairing payloads, not literally every handshake byte. The [Noise revision 34 specification](https://noiseprotocol.org/noise_rev34.html) defines the PSK processing and channel binding; it does not approve this application composition.
2. **Handshake hash.** Binding signatures to the completed handshake hash, both canonical bundles and the domain-separated pair-secret commitment ties identities and this secret to that channel, assuming canonical encoding, role checks and validation before any privileged use. Save the final hash before changing to transport mode; reject equal identities, substituted QRs, repeated secrets and confirmations from another attempt. This does not solve distributed completion; see finding 6.
3. **Library/version.** Use **`snow = "=0.10.0"` as the candidate pin for review**, with its supported 25519/ChaChaPoly/SHA256 resolver and a locked dependency graph. The [versioned Snow documentation](https://docs.rs/snow/0.10.0/snow/) documents these primitives and stateful reliable-stream transport. I verified the available version/docs, not its entire dependency security or Android compatibility; those checks and the protocol vectors remain required before choosing it for implementation.
4. **File route.** Drop it for this update unless it gets its own complete confidential exchange. “Encrypt under psk” requires both QR contributions first and a defined authenticated file/nonce/attempt lifecycle; it is not a complete design by itself. A plaintext warning does not make the new pairing contract work.
5. **Discovery tokens.** Fresh ten-minute random attempt tokens/commitments are short-lived correlators, not permanent identities by themselves. They can link broadcasts and the pairing socket during that attempt. Use only the attempt-scoped value, erase it on completion/cancellation and prohibit permanent route fallback. Today's discovery uses the commitment, not the destination token; document the actual choice. Passive observers may still infer that pairing occurred from timing and sizes.

All sensitive payloads, including profile names presently appended to BUNDLE, confirmations, bundles, pair secret and kinds 03/04, need the confidential queue rule. “Never enters outbox/carrier/relay” must be enforced by packet ownership and routing across all transports, not inferred from contents. HELLO/discovery and Noise ephemeral keys remain clear. No implementation exists from which to verify a clean capture.

### Local storage

1. **redb or SQLCipher.** redb with per-record AEAD is a reasonable continuation of the existing Rust dependency if the team accepts owning encryption, indexes, integrity failure handling and the limits in findings 8–9 and 14. SQLCipher is the established alternative when encrypting database pages and more metadata is a priority; it adds a native dependency and does not remove application quota/transaction work. There is no measured basis here to declare one faster. Keep the proposed redb direction provisional rather than introducing an unreviewed migration solely on this review.
2. **Visible metadata.** Counts, sizes, equality/grouping of opaque chat prefixes, sequence numbers, table names and access/change patterns remain visible. This is more precise than “not who”: auxiliary knowledge can associate a large or active unnamed chat with a person. Acceptability is an owner threat-model decision. For the stated seized-file threat, I recommend documenting this residual exposure explicitly and evaluating page encryption if it is unacceptable. HMAC key domains and schema encoding also need exact definitions.
3. **Carrier encryption.** Worth pursuing: it hides a seized file's envelope inventory from someone lacking the store key. Measure the CPU/I/O cost. Encrypting values means `peek_stored()` cannot keep reading plaintext fixed offsets unchanged; keeping acceptance/expiry indexes clear restores some metadata leakage, while decrypting every row on every scan restores scaling costs. Specify this choice and byte accounting. Encryption does not make stolen unlocked process state safe.
4. **Starting values.** 2 GB history, 1 GB free-space reserve, 10% fair-share floor and 1,000/hour are trial values, not validated limits. The floor does not guarantee capacity for every chat. Use byte reservations, global work bounds and control reserves before claiming audit finding 5 is fixed. Settings increases need an actual free-space check; attachments, carrier storage, staging and compaction all compete for that same free space.
5. **Incremental group events.** Do not implement the described shortcut as though `groups/` already supports it. See finding 9. Preserve replay equivalence and measure the resulting implementation against the 10,000-event target.
6. **Clock/link reports.** Rule 3 can remain advisory if the UI says nearby clocks disagree rather than asserting the local clock is wrong. Five links are not five independent people. Coarse, solicited, rate-limited reports reduce precision but do not make a characteristic offset untrackable. Rules 1–2 need finding 2 resolved; stranger warnings must never authorise pruning, stepping or lockout.

The transaction order in §4 is otherwise the right property: all libsignal mutations, accepted-message identity, history, receipt and outgoing bytes must commit together before publication. An abort must discard mutated in-memory adapters too. Exact duplicates with retained durable records can reuse results without a second history row; write transactions alone do not supply those records or filesystem atomicity. A wrong/missing wrapped key with an existing store must fail closed, including a carrier store encrypted under it. Audit finding 7's whole-snapshot cost is addressed in direction, but per-record storage alone does not bound decrypt admission or group replay cost.

### Attachments

1. **STREAM/chunks/hash.** Keep a standard library STREAM construction; 64 KiB is a plausible benchmark starting point. Specify the variant, stream nonce and final-segment semantics, with authenticated manifest hash/count/size checked together. Do not release incomplete output. See finding 15. [RustCrypto's STREAM documentation](https://docs.rs/aead-stream/latest/aead_stream/) describes segmented authenticated encryption, not this application's manifest or resume policy; pin a compatible crate family/API rather than mixing current and older module paths by name.
2. **Direct key.** Purpose separation from recognition is sensible; the proposed derivation is insufficient as a channel specification. Resolve finding 7, including direction, key epoch and replay-safe resume. A known pair secret derives all future weekly link keys, so this layer has no new post-compromise recovery; the attachment's inner file encryption and key distribution remain separate protections.
3. **Linking mesh pieces.** Yes. Pieces deliberately share the same pair-direction/hour or sender-group label as messages; timing and size add correlation. The text should not imply only statistical linking when that common label already groups traffic. Spacing alone cannot remove the common label and increases completion/expiry risk. If more privacy is required, evaluate padding, batching and a separately reviewed addressing policy; do not claim spacing fixes it. Sizes/type guesses remain possible even though exact manifest fields are encrypted.
4. **Carrier byte limit.** Storage says 64 MiB; attachments asks whether to raise it to 256 MiB. That is an open decision, not agreement. Keep 64 MiB as the specified baseline until one value is chosen and measured across docs, native config and tests. Increasing capacity does not increase the 1 MiB/min native ingress limit or solve last-copy airtime. The nominal 200 MB/800-phone calculation is an order-of-magnitude estimate under the model's contact pattern, not an attachment experiment.
5. **Starting values.** 256 KB and 60 seconds are sensible experiment ceilings, with explicit decimal/binary units and allowance for ciphertext overhead. 100 MB and 5 GB need admission/reservation, cancellation, low-space, decoder and battery tests before adoption. Twenty pending 100 MB transfers for each of 128 contacts can request 256 GB; the global cap must override that potential demand. The tests must include decompression/pixel limits, bounded thumbnails, safe filenames, MIME handling and user-authorised file opening. Android decoders in the app process are not a sandbox guarantee against crafted media.

For hidden contacts, big transfers must wait under the intended owner rule; current proposals do not yet enforce this symmetrically. Group transfer depends on a connected graph of paired members who actually have the file and participate. It cannot guarantee delivery to an unpaired isolated member. “How many confirmed” needs authenticated, deduplicated per-member completion records and expiry/retry semantics, not transport FILE_ACKs.

## Group encryption §3.11 and related edits

**Inbox publication.** A random 32-byte group-scoped inbox key can be carried in the candidate's signed join request inside confidential group traffic. The signature must cover that field, group and membership incarnation; validate the full log before using it. Strangers do not learn the secret from that encrypted publication. It is a group-shared routing/privacy secret, not a recipient public key or sender-authentication credential. Members, including a removed member holding old material, can derive labels and open the corresponding outer layer. An insider cannot silently substitute another member's signed join field without failing verification, but stale signed state can still be supplied unless local membership/version rules reject rollback. Rotated inbox updates need the same authenticated version binding. Historical logs contain earlier inbox keys; erasure claims must account for them.

**Labels.** The fixed domain, 32-byte sender and eight-byte big-endian hour make the stated HMAC input unambiguous. A random, independently scoped inbox key gives purpose separation from pair labels. Labels from the same S to R in the same hour repeat across pieces and batches. A new hour/key changes them cryptographically, but unchanged piece retries and rebatching keep their old labels/bytes until re-wrapping, and batches expose label sets, counts and lengths. Use separate per-group inbox keys or bind group/incarnation context explicitly; reusing an inbox secret across groups would correlate the same sender/recipient/hour. Include all retained versions in bounded lookup with collision handling and immediate cache updates.

**Container/parsing.** The 60,933-byte maximum in finding 16 fits the envelope. Parse count, complete directory and checked total before slicing or allocating; reject zero count, over-128 count, overflow, truncated directory, out-of-range lengths, trailing bytes and invalid aggregate size. Reject unsupported/out-of-window individual wrappers without destroying unrelated valid pieces. Define zero-length/duplicate-label entries and cap decrypt/logging work. A maximum of 128 hash-map lookups is credible with a prebuilt bounded table; it is not a bound on rebuilding all groups' tables or on native cryptographic calls.

**Finding pieces.** A newcomer with its own inbox key plus the verified sponsor log can calculate labels for existing senders before receiving their sender keys. That answers the original circular pair-secret dependency. A late member can do the same while its recipient inbox remains usable; finding 1 explains why it is not guaranteed after retention ends. Outer context and inner peer/group/recipient/generation validation must prevent installing the wrong piece. A label match alone must not confer authority. Group members do not take contact/probe slots.

**Acknowledgements.** Separating mesh receipt from encrypted generation installation is sound. A mesh ACK without install ACK means the sender must keep/retry the pending piece; the receiver may have stored only ciphertext or rejected its own piece. An authenticated install ACK may settle that recipient's pending generation even if the mesh ACK was lost; the uncertain spray allocation must still be treated conservatively as spent. It does not settle other recipients' pieces or authorise deleting another carrier's batch. Replayed install ACKs must match group, authenticated recipient/session incarnation, sender and generation. The saved-inner-digest ACK rule prevents libsignal duplicate errors being mistaken for new acceptance. Correct the universal “stored” meaning for dropped last copies and specify whether single-piece wrappers use header AD or piece AD; do not silently reuse bytes under a different AD contract.

Kind `0x02` now derives from the recipient inbox rather than a contact pair secret, so group-only bootstrap has a possible privacy root. Labels §7 and mesh §1.5 agree on that direction. Group encryption §3.6's repeated-piece explanation still refers to the old “pair's label key” and must follow the actual inbox context. One device per member is consistent across §3.1, choice 5, §3.6 and GROUP_MECHANICS §5; device removal does not revoke a copied identity, which still needs member removal and fresh identity admission.

## Corrected simulator assessment

Read the complete [Java source](../../experiments/copy-count-sim/src/CopyCountSim.java), README, FINDINGS, raw CSV and summary. The eleven configurations hold groups at 255 while varying one-to-one 12/24/48/64/96, then hold one-to-one at 96 while varying groups 32/64/128/192/255. Movement/message seeds are shared across configurations. This answers the earlier confounding of both counts.

**Hops and expiry:** the sender sends eight unchanged; a carrier send decrements it; a non-endpoint refuses zero while the intended endpoint may accept zero. Those storage/endpoint semantics match `Envelope::forwarded()`, native carrier admission and endpoint receipt of zero. However, the simulator suppresses the zero-hop transmission to a stranger before spending radio budget, unlike the private wire rule; finding 17 describes the reproduced difference. The simulator can retain a group member's zero-hop copy internally, although it cannot forward it; production must accept the piece locally without claiming zero-hop carrier custody. `run()` prunes before transfer and `offer()` checks the exact 24-hour deadline. All phones share simulated time, so future skew, working-clock disagreement and secret ageing are not modelled.

**Budgets:** floor differences across six ten-second steps give exactly 40/link direction and 100/phone incoming. These are arithmetic rate caps, not `FloodGuard`'s burst/slow-mode windows. They do not include native envelope/byte quotas, endpoint admission, stamp CPU, offer answers or total egress. “Every link stayed within limits” means the simulator enforced its own envelope counters, not that it measured radio headroom.

**Remaining gaps:** README honestly lists instant links, light load, absent battery/offline departures, slow mode, bytes, eviction, control frames, frame loss, reservations, lying peers and perfect possession knowledge. Add receipt traffic/latency (instant receipts are disclosed elsewhere), finite/persistent seen-cache behaviour, the real offer/custody state machine, packet ordering/queues, shared effective clocks and zero-hop member custody. Member-only retention is known from exact `member[]`, not proven/authenticated from real envelope bytes. It does not exercise bootstrap, inbox loss, 128-member removal bursts, attachment sizes, overload or recovery. Published gaps are substantial and mostly candid, but not complete if read as a complete target-system model.

**Reproduction:** used bundled JDK 17.0.21.1, compiled the unchanged source into the system temporary directory and ran:

```text
javac -encoding UTF-8 -d <temporary-directory> experiments/copy-count-sim/src/CopyCountSim.java
java -cp <temporary-directory> CopyCountSim --quick --only scattered --out <temporary-directory>/out
```

All eleven configurations completed. All **44 CSV rows** for seed **-1634525243** matched the corresponding stored rows exactly. For P, one-to-one delivery was 98.33%, group-30 all-member delivery 76.67%; ASK had the same percentages for that seed but different delay/sends values. A temporary Java harness against the unchanged classes passed sender-hop preservation, zero-hop carrier refusal, zero-hop endpoint acceptance, exact-expiry refusal and six-step budgets of 40/100. The full five-seed/six-scenario experiment was read and aggregated, not rerun.

A second focused harness reproduced the final-hop recipient-oracle discrepancy in finding 17 and refused transfer at the exact expiry deadline. A full five-seed rerun was also started, then stopped before completion in favour of the completed reproducible slice above; no full-rerun equivalence is claimed.

The results support trying larger copy counts under these invented worlds. Honest splitting explains limited stranger custody, and dropping a last copy prevents that recipient from further carrying it. Neither fact bounds total transmissions by 96/255: holders can contact many phones during 24 hours, members add last-copy holders, and churn creates more opportunities. Without DELIVERED, delivered carriers keep offering until expiry, eviction or copy depletion; the source stops only after its encrypted receipt. The useful bounds are lifetime, topology/honesty assumptions and each phone's explicit resource controls, not a measured network-wide airtime maximum. No optimum, operating-cost envelope or safety under hostile peers follows from this experiment.
