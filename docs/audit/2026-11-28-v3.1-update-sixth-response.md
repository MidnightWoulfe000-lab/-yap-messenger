project's own answer, not an audit.

| 1 | High | Accepted, fixed (recovery still unproven until its tests pass) | **A lost catch-up reply can be repeated.** When S accepts R's catch-up set-up, one store transaction holds the installed session, the retired queue, the replay record, the SHA-256 of the exact inner set-up and the exact reply S encrypted, with a 7-day deadline; one such record per member. A set-up whose digest matches is looked up **first**, before the allowance, the replay list and libsignal, and S re-sends the saved reply in a fresh outer layer at most once an hour, using no allowance, no replay slot and no new session, even when that member's replay list is full. A duplicate with no saved record is never treated as accepted; after the deadline a repeat is refused like any replay. Yap also saves which session it chose for catch-up and, until the first reply on it is handled, commits no message from that member that decrypts only under another session. Tests added: lost set-up, lost reply, repeated hints, a full replay list, a repeat after the deadline, restart at each step (group encryption §3.6, §3.11) |
| 2 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (with the denial-of-service limit stated) | **One work order for every set-up,** whether it names a monthly pre-key, a catch-up pre-key or an unknown one: outer layer opens; identity key belongs to an active member; saved-record lookup (an exact repeat stops here); one unit from the member's allowance of 4 attempts a day, taken whether or not the set-up succeeds; the phone-wide 64 an hour; then libsignal. The counter is per member identity across all groups and saved, so a restart does not reset it. Only a set-up libsignal authenticates writes a replay record, so a faked one cannot fill another member's list. **Stated limit:** a removed member who keeps faking set-ups can use up an allowance every day for as long as the attack goes on, and honest new sessions wait; existing sessions and saved records are unaffected. It is a denial-of-service limit, not a replay bypass, and a stronger rule needs its own reviewed design. Tests: monthly-key failures and restarts (group encryption §3.2, §3.11) |
| 3 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (per owner decision) | **Guaranteed share.** Every contact and group sender has **60 decryptions an hour** that nobody else can use up, outside the phone-wide gate. Beyond that a sender may use the shared gate (2,000 an hour per sender, 6,000 in total). Guaranteed shares together are capped at 12,000 an hour; with more than 200 active senders in an hour each share shrinks equally. So a contact who floods delays only its own receipts and key changes. The text no longer says a receipt passes "while the same contact floods" without that condition, and the generic "Rates" paragraph now defers to the essential budget and the share. Tests: three other contacts using the shared gate up; the same contact after 1,000 and 2,000 ordinary messages; the 2 GB limit; the third storage tier (local storage §5) |
| 4 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Physical cost of a write.** Each class of write has a **page allowance**: ordinary 1,024 changed pages (4 MiB), essential 64, cleanup batch 1,024, checkpoint and reclamation by `incremental_vacuum(256)` steps. Yap checks the log's growth after each statement and rolls back a transaction that passes its allowance; twice the allowance must fit before it starts, so one write cannot jump a tier. **Removing a contact, leaving a group and clearing a chat** become a small essential retirement transaction (session, pair secrets, link keys, outbox, marked "removing") followed by resumable cleanup batches; cleanup and reclamation may run down to 64 MB free, and below that Yap writes nothing and says the phone is full. Tests: a large chat deleted at each line, a restart mid-cleanup, cleanup pausing at 64 MB (local storage §5, §7) |
| 5 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Crash-safe reset and publication.** Two new intent states, **Resetting** and **Publishing**. Reset: commit Resetting with ranges cleared and the failure count raised; empty and sync the file; commit Receiving, so stale bytes never make a reset transfer look complete. Publish: commit Publishing with the final name chosen; rename and sync the folder; commit Published; at start each case (final file present, only staging file present, neither) has a stated action. The start-up sweep never deletes a file named by a Receiving, Resetting, Checking, Publishing or Deleting row. **One charge per attachment** from manifest to row gone, recounted from every row not gone. Tests: power loss before and after each step, two failed validations in a row (local storage §7a, attachments §2, §4) |
| 6 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Group-friend bootstrap has its own lifetime.** A responder keeps its exact cached kind 04 and answers a repeated kind 03 **even after it is Ready**, until the request expires. On this route the authenticated plaintext of kinds 03 and 04 carries a route binding (a fixed label, the request ID and the signed expiry) in place of the 24-hour envelope binding, and the receiver checks "the request has not expired" instead; each resend travels in a new group piece dated for its own hour, inner bytes unchanged. Ordinary envelopes keep their 24 hours. **At expiry with one side Ready:** B shows "Finishing" until any authenticated message arrives, then "Friend request may not have finished. Send a new request". Tests: kind 04 lost then responder restarted, first kind 03 after 24 hours, expiry on one phone first, the last shared group lost (pairing §5) |
| 7 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Waiting-line transitions.** The front link's turn: freed budget is held for it and builds up until it covers the whole offer (at most 64 entries), while its retries get busy answers and it keeps its place. Once enough is held it has 10 seconds to retry; if it does, its offer is answered and it **leaves** the line (a later busy answer rejoins at the back); if not, it goes to the back and the budget passes on. **Retry timers:** the 5-second retry replaces the 30, 60, 120-second back-off while in the line. Closing links leave and held budget passes on. The claim stays best-effort progress. Tests, with a deterministic scheduler: twelve 64-entry offers, arrivals and departures, the front link closing while budget is held, fast against slow links (v3.1 §1.4) |
| 8 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (with one stated 1-hour exception) | **The endpoint store always leads.** The authority never hands anyone, the carrier included, a working time more than **1 hour** past the floor last committed in the endpoint store; before it would, it commits a new endpoint floor (a small essential write about once an hour while Yap runs). Recreating an empty carrier store takes its floor from the endpoint store, so working time can go back by **at most 1 hour** after such a recreation: the one accepted exception to "never goes back", which can delay an expiry by up to an hour and cannot undo a weekly secret step. The summaries in local storage §8, mesh v3.1 and pairing now say so. Test: a carrier-only advance, then the carrier key lost and the store recreated (local storage §8) |

## Owner decisions on 28 November 2026

- **Big attachments move to a later update.** This update sends only small attachments
  (encrypted size at most 262,144 bytes). A bigger file shows "Big files are coming in
  a later update" and nothing is sent. Attachments §3 stays only as the later plan.
- **Guaranteed share (finding 3).** Every contact and group sender gets 60 decryptions
  an hour that nobody else can use up (local storage §5).

The earlier decisions of the same day, including the catch-up cost and the replay-list
cap of 256, are in [the fifth response](2026-11-28-v3.1-update-fifth-response.md).

## Still open

- **Catch-up:** the pinned-library capture and deletion experiment, and the separate
  cryptographic review. Recovery is unproven until its tests pass, including the saved
  record, simultaneous catch-up and restart.
- **Dependencies not yet checked on Android:** `snow` 0.10.0, SQLCipher through
  `rusqlite` (version not pinned) and the `aead-stream` crate family.
- **Faked set-ups** can keep a member's allowance used up (finding 2); a stronger
  admission rule would need its own reviewed design.
- **Rollback** of a restored older copy of the stores, and a wrongly confirmed forward
  clock jump, remain unsolved (local storage §8, open question 7).
- **Unmeasured starting values:** storage tiers, the 64 MB reserve, page allowances,
  guaranteed shares and gates, ordinary rates, waiting-line times and offer limits, the
  256-record cap, probe timing and carrier cost under SQLCipher.
- **Group-state shortcut:** its comparison with full derivation is still missing, so its
  performance claim cannot be used.
- **Big attachments, for the later update:** the direct channel, the `FILE_OFFER` /
  `FILE_CHUNK` / `FILE_ACK` / `FILE_DONE` encodings, the initiator message carrying the
  week, canonical PSK-info and prologue bytes, and bounded dispatch are still unwritten.
- **Earlier open items** still stand: sponsor completeness of a group log, the internet
  relay's return, real phones, and an independent expert review before any sensitive use.
