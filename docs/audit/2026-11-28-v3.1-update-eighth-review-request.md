## What to produce

### 1. Disposition of your seventh review's eight findings

A table with one row per finding (1 to 8): **answered**, **partly answered** or **not
answered**, with the evidence in the current text, as your seventh review did for the
sixth. "Answered" should mean the written defect is answered, not that it is built. The

### 2. New findings

Grade each **Critical, High, Medium or Low**. For each give:

- a **suggested fix**. Never invent cryptography: where a fix needs a new construction,

### 3. The new rules we most want checked

These are new since your last review and have had no review. Trace them as an implementer
would, and say where two sections disagree.

- **Provisional catch-up sessions and the order of selection:**
  [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md) §3.11 ("The order of
  selection", then the chosen-session rule and the saved catch-up record) and §3.6. A
  catch-up session a phone opened itself is provisional until a reply on it is handled or
  a competing set-up has been compared with it; one store transaction authenticates the
  incoming set-up, compares the two identity keys and either installs the winner or
  commits nothing. Trace both identity orders, both set-ups in flight, either arrival
  order, delayed traffic on the losing session, a repeat of a set-up already compared, and
  a restart at each boundary. Can the chosen-session rule still hold back a message from
  the winning session? Can a provisional session be left for ever?
- **Admission charges in their own transaction, and the storage §4 exception:** group §3.11
  (the failed-attempt charge) and [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md) §4.
  The per-member daily allowance and the phone-wide hourly limit commit in a small
  transaction of their own before the libsignal step and are never refunded; if the charge
  cannot be committed the set-up is dropped; a reply's rate charge is committed before the
  reply is made. Is the exception to "every error rolls back" stated tightly enough? What
  happens on a restart between the charge and the operation, when the charge itself
  fails for lack of space, and with a charge the phone cannot afford for essentials? Does
  the extra transaction cost make set-up traffic a way to wear the phone out?
- **Hourly share epochs and identity-based eligibility:** storage §5. Shares belong to
  identities; each working-time hour is an epoch in which every eligible identity (contact
  or current group member) gets the same whole share, 60 or `floor(12,000 / eligible)` if
  smaller and at least 1; someone who becomes eligible mid-hour uses the shared gate until
  the next hour. Check the arithmetic (including more than 12,000 eligible identities,
  which the minimum of 1 would break), the hour boundary, a contact who is also in several
  groups, a member added or removed mid-hour, the clock rule moving an epoch, and whether
  a late arrival can still be starved of a receipt or key change.
- **Charging a share after the outer layer opens, the separate outer-attempt budget and the
  stated group-member limit:** storage §5. A share is charged only once the outer layer
  has opened; failures go to a separate phone-wide budget of 6,000 outer attempts an hour;
  an unknown label gets no share. Is the order of checks implementable and consistent
  across contacts, groups and batches? Can the 6,000 outer-attempt budget be used up by a
  stranger so that genuine traffic is refused, and is that stated? Is the limit that a
  current or former group member can spend another member's share stated honestly, and is
  its effect on receipts and key changes bounded as the text says?
- **Page allowances worked out in advance, the frame-count check and the essential
  allowance of 320:** storage §5 (and the first storage test). The estimate is the sum of
  the maximum sizes of the rows a transaction writes or deletes, in pages, doubled, plus 16
  for indexes and accounting; a transaction over its class allowance is split or refused
  before it starts; debug builds record the frames each commit wrote and fail if any
  exceeds its estimate. Is each record type's maximum size actually fixed in the proposals?
  Do the estimate, the physical floor (64 MB), the "twice the allowance must fit" rule
  and the allowance table agree? Can a transaction that is "split" leave a half-finished
  state? Is the frame-count test able to fail for the right reason on a pinned SQLCipher
  build, and what would it have to measure that it does not say?
- **Retirement of small secrets first, sessions and blobs in cleanup batches, and its
  stated cost:** storage §7 (and §5, §7a). The first transaction deletes only small secrets
  and sets the "removing" or "leaving" mark; every read, send, receive, queued job,
  attachment step and label table checks the mark, after a restart too; the session
  (up to 512 KiB) and other blobs go later. Is every place that could use a stored session
  or blob covered by the mark? What can an attacker with the phone read from the
  surviving session before cleanup runs, and is "stored, unused" an honest description?
  What happens when cleanup is paused for lack of space, on a restart in the middle, and
  for a 128-member group, a pending outbox reservation or an attachment in progress?
- **Monthly pre-keys kept for pending friend requests:** group §3.2 and
  [encrypted pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md) §5. Old monthly pre-keys and their
  used-base-key record are kept until the later of 24 hours after replacement and the moment
  every friend request created before the replacement has expired or reached Ready: at most
  7 days longer, for at most 25 pending requests. Check which bundle kind 03 uses, a
  replacement just before delivery, kind 04 lost with a restart, crossed requests, a
  request cancelled or expired on one phone first, the last shared group lost, and whether
  keeping a pre-key longer weakens the forward secrecy claim in group §4 or the
  historical-confidentiality wording.

Also check that the earlier tests and summaries no longer contradict the chosen lifecycle,
and that the proposals agree with each other: for example the essential allowance of 320
pages wherever 64 was written before, the clock summaries in storage §8, mesh v3.1 and
pairing, the carrier store limits across storage and attachments, and whether
small-attachment transfer survives "hiding from friends".

### 4. Is the design ready to start building?

Say plainly **yes or no**. If no, list exactly what still blocks it, and separate:
- **blockers**: undefined states, transitions or failure behaviour that an implementer
  would have to invent;
- **items that can stay** as measured starting values (headroom, reserves, copy counts,
  rates, shares, gates, page allowances) or as stated limits (restored-store rollback,
  copied devices, sponsor log completeness, sustained flooding, the faked set-up limit,
  the group-member share limit, the retirement cost, the 1-hour clock exception).

**If your answer is yes,** say exactly what that does and does not mean: what you are
and are not prepared to say about the text, and why it is not an audit or a statement
that the design is secure.

**Either way,** your seventh review listed what must be checked first in implementation.
Please **confirm or correct that list**, and give it **in the order the checks should
run**, saying for each whether it must pass **before dependent work starts**. We expect at
least: `snow` 0.10.0 on Android; `rusqlite` with SQLCipher (version not yet pinned),
including the frame-count test above; the `aead-stream` crate family; and the pinned
libsignal replay, selection and recovery tests with the catch-up capture and deletion
experiment. Add or remove from that list as you see fit.

Do not mark anything as approved for building unless you are prepared to say so in so
many words. Independent expert review before sensitive use is a separate matter, and
still outstanding.
