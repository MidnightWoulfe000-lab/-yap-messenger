## What to produce

### 1. Disposition of your sixth review's nine findings

A table with one row per finding (1 to 9): **answered**, **partly answered** or **not
answered**, with the evidence in the current text, as your sixth review did for the fifth.
"Answered" should mean the written defect is answered, not that it is built. The answer
marks every finding "accepted, fixed", finding 2 as fixed "with the denial-of-service
limit stated" and finding 8 as fixed "with one stated 1-hour exception". Say whether you
agree, and whether each stated limit is narrow and honest enough.

### 2. New findings

Grade each **Critical, High, Medium or Low**. For each give:
- the locations (file, section, line);
- what is wrong, with a **concrete failure case** (inputs, state, what goes wrong);
- a **suggested fix**. Never invent cryptography: where a fix needs a new construction,
  say what property it must have and name a standard one if there is one.

### 3. The new rules we most want checked

These are new since your last review and have had no review. Trace them as an implementer
would, and say where two sections disagree.

- **The saved catch-up record, its lookup order and the chosen-session rule:**
  [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md) §3.11 (saved catch-up record,
  "keeping to the chosen session") and §3.6. One store transaction holds the installed
  session, the retired queue, the replay record, the digest of the exact set-up and the
  exact reply, with a 7-day deadline. Trace a lost set-up, a lost reply, repeated hints, a
  duplicate with a different outer layer, a full replay list, a repeat after the deadline,
  two phones catching up at once and a restart at each step. Is the order "saved record
  first, then allowance, replay list, libsignal" safe: can a forged or replayed set-up
  with a matching digest cause a reply, a slot or a session change it should not? Does
  holding back messages that decrypt only under another session leave anyone stuck?
- **One work order for every set-up, and the stated denial-of-service limit:** group §3.2
  and §3.11. Every set-up (monthly pre-key, catch-up pre-key or unknown) now passes the
  same steps, including 4 attempts a day per member identity and 64 an hour phone-wide.
  Can an honest member be locked out for longer than the text admits? Is the limit stated
  for a removed member who keeps faking set-ups honest and complete? Does anything let a
  fake set-up fill another member's replay list?
- **The guaranteed share and the work gate:** [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md)
  §5. Every contact and group sender gets 60 decryptions an hour outside the phone-wide
  gate, with the shared gate (2,000 per sender, 6,000 in all) beyond that and the shares
  capped at 12,000 an hour. Do the numbers, the shrink rule above 200 active senders and
  the order of the checks agree? Can one sender, or a crowd of unknown labels, still stop a
  receipt or key change from another sender? What does the share cost in battery and
  space in the worst case?
- **Page allowances by write class, and two-part removal:** storage §5 and §7. Ordinary
  1,024 pages, essential 64, cleanup 1,024, reclamation in `incremental_vacuum(256)`
  steps; twice the allowance must fit before a transaction starts; cleanup may run down to
  64 MB free. Removing a contact, leaving a group and clearing a chat become a small
  essential retirement transaction followed by resumable cleanup batches. Do the
  allowances, the physical floor and the admission order agree? Is anything left readable
  or sendable between the two parts? What happens on restart in the middle, and when the
  phone is full?
- **Resetting and Publishing, and one charge per attachment:** storage §7a and
  [attachments](../proposals/attachments-v1.md) §2 and §4. Power loss before and after
  each step of reset and of publication; the three start-up cases for publication (final
  file present, only staging file present, neither); the sweep that never deletes a file
  named by a live row; the single charge from manifest to row gone. Does each state have a
  defined exit? Can the charge be counted twice or not at all?
- **The friend-bootstrap route binding, the Ready responder's window and the one-side-Ready
  outcome:** [encrypted pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md) §5. A responder keeps
  its cached kind 04 and answers a repeated kind 03 even after Ready, until the request
  expires; kinds 03 and 04 carry a route binding with the request ID and signed expiry
  instead of the 24-hour envelope binding; a repeat travels in a new group piece. Check
  replay after Ready, a request that expires on one phone first, B showing "Finishing",
  the last shared group lost, crossed requests, and whether the route binding can be
  confused with an ordinary envelope's binding or used across requests.
- **The waiting-line transitions:** [mesh v3.1](../proposals/local-mesh-v3.1-group-envelopes.md)
  §1.4. Held budget for the front link, the 10-second window, leaving after a served
  offer, going to the back on a miss, the 5-second retry replacing the back-off, closing
  links. Is it an implementable service rule for whole 64-entry offers? Is the
  best-effort claim stated honestly? Can a slow or hostile link keep the front for long
  without ever being served?
- **The endpoint-leads clock rule and its 1-hour exception:** storage §8. The authority
  never hands anyone a working time more than 1 hour past the last endpoint floor; a new
  endpoint floor is committed about hourly; a recreated carrier store takes its floor from
  the endpoint store, so time can go back by at most 1 hour. Check startup order across the
  two stores, what a friend, two friends or a stranger can do with the 1-hour slack, and
  whether the exception can undo a weekly secret step or extend any expiry beyond what the
  text says.

Also check that the earlier tests and summaries no longer contradict the chosen lifecycle,
and that the proposals agree with each other: for example the carrier store limits across
storage and attachments, the clock summaries in storage §8, mesh v3.1 and pairing, and
whether small-attachment transfer survives "hiding from friends".

### 4. Is the design ready to start building?

Say plainly **yes or no**. If no, list exactly what still blocks it, and separate:
- **blockers**: undefined states, transitions or failure behaviour that an implementer
  would have to invent;
- **items that can stay** as measured starting values (headroom, reserves, copy counts,
  rates, the new guaranteed-share totals and page allowances) or as stated limits
  (restored-store rollback, copied devices, sponsor log completeness, sustained flooding,
  the faked set-up limit, the 1-hour clock exception).

**If your answer is yes,** say exactly what that does and does not mean: what you are
and are not prepared to say about the text, and why it is not an audit or a statement
that the design is secure. Say which **pinned-library tests and dependency checks must
come first in implementation**, before any dependent work starts. We already know of
`snow` 0.10.0, SQLCipher through `rusqlite` (version not pinned), the `aead-stream`
crate family and the catch-up capture and deletion experiment; add or remove from that
list as you see fit.

Do not mark anything as approved for building unless you are prepared to say so in so
many words. Independent expert review before sensitive use is a separate matter, and
still outstanding.

## How to write the report

Follow the format of [your sixth review](2026-11-28-v3.1-update-sixth-review.md):
- reviewer, date, the revision you read (HEAD, and whether the working tree was clean for

  failure case and a suggested fix;

- **verification limits**: say plainly what you checked by reading, by arithmetic and by
  running something, and what you did not (for example an Android build, interoperability
  tests, power-loss tests, fuzzing, radio or energy measurement);
- **SHA-256 fingerprints** of every input you read, as your sixth review did, so we can
  tell which text you saw.
