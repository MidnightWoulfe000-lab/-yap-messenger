# Response to the seventh review of the v3.1 update

Answers [Codex's seventh review of the v3.1 update](2026-11-28-v3.1-update-seventh-review.md)
of 28 November 2026 (seven Medium, one Low, no Critical or High). It follows
[the response to the sixth review](2026-11-28-v3.1-update-sixth-response.md). Revised the
same day: [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md),
[local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md), [encrypted
pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md) and
[photos, voice notes and files](../proposals/attachments-v1.md). Nothing is implemented.
"Fixed" means the written proposal now says something different, not that it has been
built, tested or checked by anyone else, and every changed proposal needs another
review. This is the project's own answer, not an audit.

| # | Grade | Status | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Order of catch-up selection.** A catch-up session a phone opened itself is *provisional* until a reply on it is handled or a competing set-up has been compared with it. When an authenticated catch-up set-up arrives while a provisional session to that member exists, one store transaction authenticates it, compares the two by identity key, and either installs the incoming winner (archiving the provisional session, retiring its queue, saving the catch-up record and reply) or commits nothing and keeps waiting on the provisional winner. The chosen-session rule applies only after this selection, so it can never block the winner. Tests: both identity orders, both set-ups in flight, either arrival order, delayed losing-session traffic, a restart at each boundary (group encryption §3.11) |
| 2 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **The charge is its own transaction.** The failed-attempt charge (per-member daily allowance and phone-wide hourly limit) commits in a small transaction of its own *before* the libsignal step and is never refunded. The libsignal step then runs all-or-nothing as before: a refusal discards every session, pre-key, history and outbox change but keeps the charge. If the charge cannot be committed, the set-up is dropped without libsignal work. A reply's rate charge is committed before the reply is made. Local storage §4's all-errors-roll-back rule gains this one stated exception. Tests: invalid monthly, catch-up and unknown-key set-ups with a restart straight after the charge and straight after the refusal (group encryption §3.11, local storage §4) |
| 3 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **How shares are set.** Shares belong to identities, so a person who is a contact and in several groups has one. Each working-time hour is an epoch: at its start the phone counts eligible identities (contacts and current group members) and gives each the same whole number, 60 or `floor(12,000 / eligible)` if smaller, at least 1, so shares are fixed in advance and never total more than 12,000. Someone who becomes eligible mid-hour uses only the shared gate until the next hour, and nothing already used is taken back. Tests: 200 fully served senders then a 201st, membership changes, hour boundaries, more eligible identities than the budget covers (local storage §5) |
| 4 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (with the stated limit) | **What a share is charged for.** A label is visible to anyone and proves nothing, so a share is charged only after the outer layer has opened. Envelopes whose outer layer fails are charged to a separate phone-wide budget of 6,000 outer attempts an hour, never to a sender's share, and an unknown label gets no share. **Stated limit:** for contacts, only that contact can spend its share (the outer key comes from the pair secret); for group traffic the outer layer proves only knowledge of an inbox key, so a current or former group member can spend another member's share by naming them, until libsignal's inner check fails. Like faked set-ups, this is a denial-of-service limit, not a way in. Tests: copied labels with failed outer tags, forged inner identities, unknown labels, genuine receipts after each budget is used up (local storage §5) |
| 5 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (enforcement unproven until its test passes) | **The allowance is worked out before the transaction,** because watching the log cannot work (SQLite can keep changed pages in cache and write them at commit). Every record type has a fixed maximum size; the estimate is the sum of the maximum sizes of the rows it writes or deletes, in pages, doubled, plus 16 for indexes and accounting; a transaction over its class allowance is split or refused before it starts. Whether this really bounds pages written, with cache spill and reused log space, is the **first storage test** on the pinned SQLCipher build: debug builds record the frames each commit wrote (SQLite's write-ahead-log hook) and fail if any commit exceeds its estimate (local storage §5) |
| 6 | Medium | Accepted, fixed (with the stated cost) | **Retirement is bounded.** The first transaction deletes only small secrets (pair secrets, link keys; for a group, inbox and sender-key secrets), cancels the outbox, raises the recognition generation and sets the "removing" or "leaving" mark. Every read, send, receive, queued job, attachment step and label table checks the mark, after a restart too. The session (up to 512 KiB, one batch of its own), other blobs and history go in resumable cleanup batches. **Stated cost:** until cleanup runs, which may wait for space, the session stays stored, unused. The essential allowance is raised from 64 to 320 pages (1.25 MiB), enough to rewrite one maximum-size session. Tests: a maximum-size session, leaving a full 128-member group, pending outbox reservations, a restart while cleanup is paused (local storage §5, §7) |
| 7 | Medium | Accepted, fixed | **Pre-keys are kept for pending friend requests.** The contact set-up uses the responder's monthly bundle current in the group log when the initiator builds kind 03. Old monthly pre-keys and their used-base-key record are kept until the later of 24 hours after replacement and the moment every friend request created before the replacement has expired or reached Ready: at most 7 days longer, for at most 25 pending requests. No new key exchange; an existing pre-key just lives longer. Tests: a request made just before a monthly replacement with kind 03 first delivered 25 hours later, kind 04 lost and a restart, crossed requests, the last shared group lost (group encryption §3.2, pairing §5) |
| 8 | Low | Accepted, fixed | **Stale summaries.** Attachments §4 admission now says small attachments only in this update, and the expiry rule for incomplete big transfers is marked "later update". The attachments tests check the small ceiling (262,144 accepted, 262,145 not) and that every big manifest is refused with nothing reserved; the 100 MiB boundary and the direct-transfer tests sit under a "later update" heading. The storage test for removing a contact now checks denial of use and a durable cleanup mark at retirement and after a restart, and no record, orphan or dangling reference only after cleanup completes. Other summaries were checked for an essential allowance of 64 pages or deletion of the session in the first transaction; none remained. The status line of each changed proposal mentions this review and needs another review |

## Other points in the review

| Point | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Big attachments | Codex agrees that deferring them is effective and removes the unwritten direct channel from this update's dependencies. Attachments §3 stays as the later plan, and the review does not ask for it to be reviewed now |
| Desktop SQLite checks | Using SQLite 3.54.1 with 4,096-byte pages, Codex found the write-ahead log at 0 before commit, 0 after an INSERT of 1 MiB and 1,062,992 bytes after COMMIT, and 130 frames for deleting one 512 KiB session with `secure_delete` on. These are ordinary SQLite demonstrations, not a pinned SQLCipher or Android result; they are the reason for findings 5 and 6 |
| Work arithmetic | 200 × 60 guaranteed plus 6,000 shared is at most 18,000 decryptions an hour, about five a second, and about 1.10 GiB an hour of 64 KiB envelopes presented for classification. These are work ceilings, not battery, CPU or storage measurements. The storage test for a full shared gate is changed from three contacts × 2,000 to each also using its 60 guaranteed (2,060 each), since 3 × 2,000 does not use the 6,000 up (local storage §5) |
| First implementation checks | Codex lists what must be done before relying on the design: `snow` on Android, the `rusqlite`/SQLCipher combination, the `aead-stream` crate family, and the pinned-libsignal replay and recovery tests |
| Simulator and size program | The simulator's 44 rows matched again. The size program still did not build on the reviewer's Windows machine, so its figures are still not reproduced by anyone but the project |

## Still open

- **Feasibility and dependency checks** Codex lists: `snow` 0.10.0 on Android, SQLCipher
  through `rusqlite` (version not pinned), the `aead-stream` family, and pinned-libsignal
  replay, selection and recovery tests, including the page-allowance test.
- **Catch-up:** the capture and deletion experiment and the separate cryptographic
  review. Recovery is unproven until its tests pass.
- **Two stated denial-of-service limits:** spoofed set-ups using up a member's daily
  allowance (finding 2 of the sixth review), and a current or former group member
  spending another member's work share (finding 4). Stronger rules would need their own
  reviewed design.
- **Rollback** of a restored older copy of the stores, and a wrongly confirmed forward
  clock jump (local storage §8).
- **Unmeasured starting values:** storage tiers, the 64 MB reserve, page allowances,
  shares, gates and rates, waiting-line times, the 256-record cap, probe timing and
  carrier cost under SQLCipher.
- **Group-state shortcut:** its comparison with full derivation is still missing.
- **Big attachments, for the later update:** the direct channel, `FILE_*` encodings and
  dispatch are still unwritten.
- **Earlier open items** still stand: sponsor completeness of a group log, the internet
  relay's return, real phones, and an independent expert review before any sensitive use.
