## Findings

### 1. Medium — the minimum-one share contradicts the 12,000 total

**Locations:** [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md) §5, lines 260–268.

**Failure case:** the phone has 12,001 distinct eligible identities across its contacts and groups at an epoch boundary. `floor(12,000 / 12,001)` is zero, but “at least 1” assigns each identity one decryption: 12,001 in total. Larger eligible populations increase the excess. The proposals cap contacts and members per group, but do not cap the union of identities across all groups at 12,000. The 2 GB byte limit is not that identity-count limit.

For 201 eligible identities the revised rule works: 59 each, total 11,859, with unused budget left over. The defect is specifically the zero-share boundary, not the previous active-set recalculation problem. The empty eligible set also needs an explicit no-allocation case, rather than division by zero.

**Suggested fix:** state the saturation policy. Choose whether to cap eligible identities, permit zero allocations with a defined service/rotation rule, or change the total guarantee, and specify the user-visible consequence. Do not silently discard eligible identities or overrun the cap. Test 0, 1, 200, 201, 12,000 and 12,001 identities, including a late joiner and the next epoch. The numerical budgets can remain starting values; their overflow behaviour cannot.

### 2. Medium — failed-outer charging does not define admission before the attempted decrypt

**Locations:** storage §4, lines 154–160; §5, lines 251–290; [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md) §3.11, lines 775–797, especially its 32-outer-attempt batch ceiling.

**Failure case:** a stranger copies recognised destination labels and supplies validly stamped envelopes with invalid outer tags. After 6,000 failed outer attempts, a genuine contact envelope arrives with an unused guaranteed share. The phone cannot know that its outer layer will succeed without trying it. Refusing before the attempt enforces the public budget but denies that genuine envelope; trying first and charging only failures permits an attempt after the budget was exhausted. The text does not choose between those behaviours, reserve a trial token, or describe any refund/transfer rule consistent with durable non-refundable admission charges.

The unit is also unclear. An incoming batch can require up to 32 outer candidate decryptions in total; a tag collision can require several candidate keys. “6,000 outer attempts ... one cheap AEAD each” cannot be implemented by charging one failed envelope after all its candidates have been tried. Nor can an unknown label create a free share just to obtain a trial.

The group-member limitation is materially honest: current members, and former members retaining fallback inboxes, can generate valid outer traffic claiming another member and exhaust that identity's inner-work opportunity. This can delay its receipts and key changes for as long as the attack continues. It is not bounded to one hour over the lifetime of an attack. A global outer cap, if checked before all trials, introduces another limitation: a stranger can deny classification to everyone until capacity returns. That outcome is not currently stated beside the guarantee.

**Suggested fix:** define one ordered admission contract for label lookup, reserving actual outer AEAD attempts, trial/collision/batch accounting, successful outer attribution, durable share/shared-gate charging and inner processing. State what happens when each budget is exhausted, including whether an outer trial can occur at all and whether any reservation is released. Account for outer openings already done before the mesh ACK rather than opening them twice. Either accept and disclose global outer-gate denial by strangers, or specify the stronger bounded mechanism required; do not claim unconditional contact isolation from the current text. Test a genuine receipt immediately after 6,000 failed trials, multiple candidates and directory-only/current-label batches.

### 3. Medium — the estimate assumes unspecified row bounds and permits unspecified atomic splits

**Locations:** storage §3, lines 104–126; §4, lines 145–165; §5, lines 198–220; §§6–7, lines 322–368. Code context: `groups/src/state.rs`, `GroupState` and `derive()`; group §3.6 and §5's storage/hand-over rules.

**Failure case:** a long-lived group has many signed proposals, revocations, elections and warnings. The proposed `group_state` row holds derived state, but has no encoded-byte maximum or defined overflow representation. The existing `GroupState` has maps/vectors of this material; a 128-current-member cap does not bound its accumulated governance state to an ordinary 1,024-page transaction. Saying that “every record type has a fixed maximum” supplies neither that maximum nor behaviour when valid state exceeds it. The same admission estimate needs bounds for encoded sender-key state, pending/cached pieces, request records and their indexes, not only the session and envelope limits already known.

An oversized operation may then be “split or refused”. Cleanup has durable intent and may be split; cryptographic operations must still commit session/pre-key mutations with their output. For example, processing group-carried friend bootstrap can touch the group session and the new contact session. Two permitted 512 KiB session rows alone produce an estimate of 528 pages under the stated doubled-page sum plus 16, exceeding the essential 320-page allowance. Splitting into independent commits without a durable intermediate message/intent can advance one session and lose the input needed by the other after a crash. Refusal is safe, but the retry cannot make an intrinsically oversized operation fit.

Retirement needs the same aggregate accounting. Cancelling a potentially large outbox must be a bounded logical cancellation through the retirement mark or bounded cleanup, not an unbounded rewrite of every envelope in the first commit. A full group's inbox/sender-key secrets and skipped-key material must be included in that first transaction's estimate; calling each secret small is not a bound on their total. The session-blob move fixed the earlier specific defect, but does not prove all remaining retirement writes fit.

**Suggested fix:** supply a bounded record/operation contract or explicitly make its completion a first schema-feasibility gate before dependent work. Define encoded maxima, index/overflow costs and the handling of valid larger derived state. Name operations that must remain atomic and may only refuse; for multi-stage operations, define the durable input/intent, acknowledgements, restart action and continuous quota ownership before allowing a split. State logical outbox cancellation and which group key material is erased immediately versus deferred. Validate whole operations against their class, not just one maximum session. This does not require inventing cryptography, but it does require a reviewable storage lifecycle.

### 4. Medium — the WAL-hook validation needs per-transaction and post-commit semantics

**Locations:** storage §5, lines 206–209, and its failed-commit rule at lines 239–241.

**Failure case:** a debug test compares the hook's frame argument directly with a transaction's estimate. If the WAL already contains 900 frames and a small transaction appends two, the hook reports the WAL's current total, 902, not two. The test can fail a correctly bounded transaction for the wrong reason. Conversely, naive subtraction across a WAL reset/reuse can miss work or produce a negative delta. A commit-only counter also does not measure an aborted transaction's peak spill/space consumption.

The [SQLite WAL-hook contract](https://www.sqlite.org/c3ref/wal_hook.html) says the callback runs after commit, reports pages currently in the WAL including earlier commits, and may return an error even though the commit has occurred. Treating that error as an ordinary failed commit and discarding the in-memory result under the general rollback rule is therefore wrong. Registering autocheckpoint settings can replace the callback too; a test with no active observation must not pass. [SQLite's WAL documentation](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html) describes WAL reset/reuse, which the accounting must handle.

**Suggested fix:** define the test observable and pass condition: per-database/connection transaction boundaries, WAL generation/reset tracking, actual appended frames, spill/rollback peak writes and physical space needed by the failure path. Where the hook cannot measure an observable, use validated additional instrumentation on the pinned build. Keep assertions in the test harness; do not use a post-commit hook error as an application rollback mechanism. Verify that the callback is installed and receives known commits, and preserve explicit checkpoint behaviour. A passed test must show the *whole-operation estimate* bounds the intended physical work, not merely that a cumulative callback value is below an arbitrary threshold.

### 5. Medium — retention based on known request creation does not pin the bundle used by a delayed peer

**Locations:** group §3.2, lines 172–183; [encrypted pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md) §5, lines 223–248 and 249–259; signed time checks at lines 308–312.

**Failure case A, unknown request:** A creates a friend request just before B replaces its monthly bundle, but B has not received that request. B has no pending request to extend retention for, so it deletes the old private pre-keys 24 hours after replacement. The request reaches B later and is accepted within its signed seven-day window. A still has the old group-log bundle and builds/retries kind 03 with it. B can authenticate the request, but cannot reconstruct a private key already deleted. A condition about “every friend request created before replacement” cannot make B know an undelivered remote request.

**Failure case B, stale log:** A creates its request *after* B's replacement, while A's log still shows B's old monthly bundle. Even if B knows the request before deleting the key, its creation is not before the replacement, so the stated extension does not cover it. The rule selecting whichever bundle is current in the initiator's log does not establish a shared bundle choice. Group partitions and delayed logs are part of the design, not exceptional misuse.

The “at most 7 days longer” claim also needs its clock basis: B accepts a signed request whose created time is up to two days ahead of B's working time. Its signed expiry can then be nearly nine days ahead locally. Counting only 25 known pending requests bounds their number, not knowledge of other requests or this time discrepancy.

**Suggested fix:** bind the actual responder bundle ID/hash to the accepted request through the existing authenticated group exchange, and ensure the responder promises retention through that request's agreed expiry *before* the initiator uses it. State which message supplies that binding for both identity orders, and what happens if a stale or already deleted bundle is named. Alternatively choose an explicitly reviewed refusal/retry limit and narrow the seven-day recovery promise. Retain used-base-key records with the selected key; a Ready responder still uses its saved exact kind 04 for retries. Test unknown-at-replacement requests, stale logs, allowed clock skew, crossed requests, cancellation on one phone, last-route loss and restart.

Keeping monthly private pre-keys longer also lengthens the later-compromise exposure of recorded monthly setup traffic. Those setups can carry sender-key distributions, unlike catch-up's deliberately restricted first payload. Document that difference and include it in the existing capture/deletion experiment and confidentiality review. This review does not claim that retained monthly keys alone expose every subsequent ratchet message. The old-ciphertext claim must remain conditional on erasure of every still-sufficient decryption path, not merely deletion of a sender-key row.

### 6. Low — the later-update heading now excludes current safety tests

**Locations:** [attachments](../proposals/attachments-v1.md), test plan lines 282–292.

**Failure case:** the new heading “Later update tests (not part of this update)” precedes the future 100 MiB/direct-transfer tests, but its scope also encloses the listener-privacy test, “Recognition ... starts no transfer in this update”, chat-file deletion and crafted image/audio decoder-limit tests. A checklist following that heading can skip current small-attachment safety/deletion checks. The declaration about no transfer in this update directly contradicts its placement in a future-only block.

**Suggested fix:** close the future-only block after its own channel/large-file cases, or use a separate heading/list for current tests. Keep small-file privacy, no direct-channel dispatch, cleanup and decoder-resource checks in this update. The admission wording and size-ceiling sentence identified last round were otherwise corrected. This is a scope-documentation defect, not a request to review deferred attachments §3.

## Requested trace assessments

### Catch-up selection and repeated comparisons

The new provisional distinction resolves the specific former circular wait. If both phones initiate, the larger identity's phone authenticates and installs the smaller identity's incoming winner, retires its own losing queue and saves a reply; the smaller identity's phone discards the competing losing setup's staged mutations and waits on its own session. Reverse arrival order changes neither intended winner. Only the initiator's other-session traffic is held pending its first reply; incoming competing setups explicitly use selection instead of that guard. A phone accepting the winner has no initiator guard to wait on.

A repeated losing setup which was authenticated only for comparison was not committed, so it is not an accepted replay-cache hit and cannot install a session merely because it was compared earlier. Repeating it may spend another setup attempt and repeat bounded work; it must not persist a used-base-key mark from a rolled-back comparison. The separately committed admission charge remains. The seven-day retry/deadline prevents a provisional attempt waiting forever: after it the app reports out-of-step/re-invitation. A delayed losing-session message must not resurrect retired queued work or override the winner merely because libsignal can decrypt an archived state. The identity-order selection rule, saved chosen base and ordinary duplicate handling must be exercised together in the pinned tests, including a losing setup arriving after the winning reply.

A lost accepted setup reply is still answered from the saved exact digest/reply record before fresh-setup allowance, replay-list saturation or libsignal. Rewrapping changes the outer layer, not that digest. Replay of the identical accepted setup may elicit the bounded cached encrypted reply but creates no slot/session; the reply goes to its original member/session context, never a replay-supplied destination. After the record deadline, no cache hit or duplicate error authorises fresh acceptance: retained used-base state continues to refuse reconstruction. The full-list accepted-repeat case remains covered. Restart must preserve provisional/chosen phase, retry bytes/deadline, queue retirement, accepted record and reply in the stated transaction boundaries. These are text-level conclusions; none of these library traces ran successfully here.

### Admission charges, identity epochs and exhaustion

The new exception is appropriately narrow: only admission/rate counters commit separately. A crash after a charge but before libsignal consumes work credit without doing the work, which reduces availability but cannot advance a ratchet. A failure of the charge itself performs no expensive libsignal step and emits no response. At logical ordinary-storage saturation, counter writes classified as essential may use their reserve; below the essential physical line, they cannot, so setup/reply work pauses or drops. It must not bypass the floor to serve essentials or fall back to uncharged work. A counter commit is not proof that the following reply or setup operation completed; restart still needs its own durable pending/cache state.

For setup charges alone the phone-wide ceiling is 64/hour; the catch-up answer ceiling is ten/hour. Those yield at most 1,776 hourly-budget charge transactions per day if each attempt/answer needs one transaction, before other work-gate charges. Rejected attempts after a cap is exhausted should not manufacture new rows or keep writing the same exhausted counter. Work/share/failed-outer counters can cost much more under their larger ceilings. Persistent counters plus FULL synchronisation can be a substantial write/battery cost even when no message is accepted. Measure frames, syncs, bytes and energy at the stated ceilings on the pinned store; the counts establish limits, not a wear guarantee. The numerical rates may remain starting values.

Eligibility now belongs to distinct identities across all groups and contacts, fixed for each working-time hour. Removing one group role mid-hour does not remove an identity still eligible through another role; losing its last role must stop authorising its traffic, without recycling its spent share into new identities. A new identity during an epoch may be starved of classification by a full shared gate until the next epoch—an explicit, bounded initial delay in the proposed allocation, not an immediate guarantee. The epoch's eligible set and spent counters need durable identity/epoch keys so restart cannot mint the same allocation again. A one-hour carrier-recreation clock setback must reuse an already visited epoch's accounting; a forward jump can skip epochs but must not refill an old one when the clock is corrected. Finding 1 concerns the remaining saturation policy.

With at most 12,000 guaranteed and 6,000 shared inner decryptions, the intended ceiling remains 18,000/hour, about five/second, after outer admission. For 201 identities the equal share is 59 and the guaranteed total 11,859. A contact who is also in several groups has one allocation. A group member with inbox material can still exhaust that identity's single allocation, including work that might otherwise classify its contact receipt; it must not be described as contact-only isolation for such a dual-role identity. Neither group-member spoofing nor stranger/global-gate saturation is an inner-authentication bypass. Both can affect availability, and the promises/tests must state their scope.

The total outer-work ceiling cannot yet be computed from the prose in finding 2: it depends on whether 6,000 counts failures, pre-reserved trials or envelopes, and how successful/batch candidates are charged. Once that contract is explicit, distinguish candidate AEADs, inner libsignal calls and committed records. The 2 GB/5 GB logical quotas, 64 MB logical reserve, carrier ingress limits and physical tiers remain independent constraints. A decrypt allowance alone does not reserve essential bytes or guarantee an accepted key change after its own commit quota is exhausted.

### Write estimates, essential allowance and retirement

The 320-page essential allowance consistently replaces the former 64-page allowance. Assuming 4 KiB pages, it is 1.25 MiB; a single 512 KiB session contributes 128 pages, doubled to 256 plus 16 = 272 under the proposed estimate. That fits 320 in isolation. Two such rows contribute 528 and do not. The estimate must include all changed/deleted records and index/metadata work, with per-record rounding and the actual pinned page size. The “plus 16” is a proposed overhead bound to validate, not a mathematical theorem about arbitrary schemas/B-trees.

The separate physical reservation doubles the *class allowance* again: an essential write needs 640 pages, about 2.5 MiB, above its `1 GB + 32 MB` line; a cleanup write needs 2,048 pages, about 8 MiB, above 64 MB free. At exactly 64 MB there is no headroom for a new cleanup transaction. Checkpoint/reclamation must account for any database/WAL growth while relocating pages, not presume that every intermediate step immediately releases space. These qualifications reconcile the table and strict pre-reservation rule; neither another app consuming disk nor failed I/O is guaranteed recoverable without freeing space.

Retirement now has an explicit denial mark covering reads, sends, receives, queued workers, attachment steps and label/recognition work after restart. That is a substantive improvement. Pending outbox allocations must be logically cancelled at retirement, unsent reservations released safely and possibly sent reservations treated under the existing spent/uncertain rules; cleanup must not release copies already sent. Attachment jobs must not publish or recreate a retired chat after the mark. A full group's cleanup can take many transactions, with each session blob its own batch, and may pause for space. Clearing history keeps its own resumable cleanup semantics.

The stated cost is honest if read as an app-enforced denial: the session remains extractable from an unlocked or decrypted store until cleanup runs. It may contain ratchet keys capable of opening recorded or later session traffic according to that session's state; retaining it is not equivalent to cryptographic erasure. Pair-secret deletion removes the honest app's future label/recognition derivation, but does not erase a session blob or a previously copied store. Full-group immediate secret deletion and outbox cancellation still need the aggregate bound/representation in finding 3. No flash-erasure guarantee follows from secure deletion and incremental reclamation.

### Monthly pre-key retention and confidentiality

Where the responder knows the request and pins the actual bundle before replacement, retaining that bundle's private keys and used-base records can repair the former 25-hour first-delivery trace. Once the responder accepted kind 03 and generated cached kind 04, loss of kind 04 is repaired from that cache even after local Ready; it does not require re-consuming the pre-key. Crossed requests must retain the winner's selected bundle and signed expiry; cancelling/expiring a local non-Ready attempt must not silently drop key retention needed by another live request. The present creation-time rule does not supply all those associations, as finding 5 shows.

Longer monthly retention does not itself change libsignal's primitives, but preserves a decryption path for some recorded setup traffic for longer. The restricted catch-up first-payload claim applies to catch-up keys, not automatically to ordinary monthly setups carrying a hand-over. The capture/deletion experiment must distinguish those paths, subsequent ratchet progress, erased sender generations, cached distribution material and retained pre-keys. Keep §4's protection conditional on the actual key-erasure boundary and separately review the composition. No claim that every later message is exposed or protected has been established here.

### Clock, recognition, attachments and deferred scope

The one-authority clock policy is unchanged from round seven. Startup opens/reconciles both readable stores before expiry, label generation or pruning. The endpoint's floor must advance before a value more than one hour ahead is handed out; failed floor commits cannot authorise dependent work beyond that bound. Recreating only the carrier can therefore lose at most one hour per recreation. It cannot restore a committed weekly secret step or deleted endpoint row, since those commit with the endpoint floor. Repeated local losses can cause repeated bounded setbacks; this is not a lifetime drift bound. Restoring both stores remains the separate rollback limit.

A friend, two colluding friends or a stranger can cause only a time-disagreement note through link replies. They cannot directly lower the authoritative floor, recreate its store or confirm a system-clock jump. The new admission epochs must respect this existing clock exception rather than treating a repeated wall-hour label as a fresh epoch. Re-pairing still does not reset global working time. These are specification conclusions, not an executed clock/recovery result.

Recognition still only names a link row, under the generation/week check and filler fallback. Hiding from a friend withdraws outgoing genuine tags while preserving incoming matching. A stalled table worker, stale queued probe and immediate reconnect must not bypass the final send permission check. Timing, shuffling, queue cancellation and publication remain unmeasured. Small attachment carrier traffic is unaffected by hiding, because it depends on labels/outer wrapping, not recognition. No direct transfer starts in this update.

The Receiving/Checking/Resetting/Publishing/Published/Deleting attachment lifecycle and one-charge rule are unchanged. Range ACK follows synced bytes and committed ranges. Reset intent clears ranges before truncation; publication intent records the final name before rename and directory sync. Startup completes final-present, staging-only and neither-present cases without sweeping a live intended filename. A second validation failure deletes through the stated cleanup/error path. Every live attachment row retains its full single charge until gone, including paused cleanup. The round-seven desktop checks concerned SQLite WAL growth, not these filesystem transitions; no power-loss test has run here.

The small encrypted-size arithmetic remains: 0 plaintext bytes give one segment/16 encrypted bytes; 65,536 gives one/65,552; 65,537 gives two/65,569; 262,080 gives four/262,144. A 262,081-byte plaintext exceeds the ceiling after tags. Five 60 KiB transport pieces suffice for the maximum small file, independently of STREAM's 64 KiB segment boundaries. These are arithmetic checks, not STREAM interoperability or decoder validation.

**Deferred-channel scope check:** mesh “One update” and §§3.1/3.3/3.9 still defer big transfers; the frame list at lines 841–845 leaves types 13/14 unused. Attachments §§1/4/6 refuse big manifests, reserve nothing for them and queue nothing big. UI wording still says big files are coming later. Storage's 5 GB aggregate attachment quota can hold many small files and does not activate direct transfer; carrier limits still agree at 10,000 envelopes and 64 MiB of envelope bytes, with the existing 1 MiB/minute native intake limit. No new rule depends on the unwritten direct channel or `FILE_*` encodings. Attachments §3 was not reviewed. The only new scope leftover found outside it is the test-block placement in finding 6.
