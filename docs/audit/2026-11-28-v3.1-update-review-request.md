# Review request: the v3.1 update, third round

**From:** the project owner, 28 November 2026.
**For:** Codex, or any reviewer.

Please review five proposals and one simulator that ship in the same update. Nothing in
them is built yet. Write your findings to a new file,
`docs/audit/2026-11-28-v3.1-update-review.md`, and change no other file.

Two of the proposals have been reviewed twice already, so for those this is a **third
review**: check that our changes answer your findings, and look for problems the
changes bring in. Three proposals are new, so for those it is a **first review**.

## What to review

### A. Third review

1. [Local mesh v3.1](../proposals/local-mesh-v3.1-group-envelopes.md), all three parts
   (group delivery, message routes, friend names), and its "Also in this update" list.
2. [Rotating address labels](../proposals/rotating-address-labels.md).

Check them against:
- [your second review](2026-11-27-local-mesh-v3.1-second-review.md) (nine High, six
  Medium); and
- [our answers](2026-11-27-local-mesh-v3.1-second-response.md), which say for each
  finding whether we accepted it, what changed, and what stays open.

Go **finding by finding** (1 to 15). For each, say whether the change answers it fully,
partly or not at all, and why. Findings 8 and 9 (the emergency wipe) are marked "no
longer applies" because the wipe draft was withdrawn; say if you disagree. Findings 7 and 13
(a newcomer's first piece, and the batch container) are now marked fixed and are covered
in section D below.

Then look for **new problems the changes introduce**. These are the changes that have
had no review at all:
- **The 70-byte binding** (v3.1 §1.1): offsets, the class and starting-copies fields, the
  hop byte at encoded byte 70, and the two outer-layer descriptions moved to 70 bytes.
- **The last-copy rule with `DELIVERED` retired** (§1.2 to §1.4): a last copy goes to
  every phone met, and nobody answers whether an envelope is theirs. What do `WANT` and a
  member re-offering a last copy still reveal? Is it enough that every phone sends the
  same `ACK`?
- **`OFFER`/`WANT` and the copy reservation table** (§1.4): the states Not offered,
  Offered, Sending, Uncertain and Settled, the timeouts, offer IDs, atomic reservation,
  uncertain transfers counted as spent, stored counts, and cursor paging. Can copies be
  lost, duplicated or counted twice, or state grow without bound?
- **Weekly-stepped pair secrets** (labels §1): a secret made during pairing and stepped
  forward each week by HKDF, with nothing sent. Is it sound? Check the week boundary and
  clock dependency, a phone that is off for weeks, and what a copied phone can do.
- **Padded recognition work** (v3.1 §3.2 to §3.4): always 128 HMACs to make a probe and
  256 expected tags to check one, the caps, and "a second `PROBE` leaves the first
  result". Does the work really not depend on the number of contacts, and can a stranger
  still learn anything from timing or from the caps running out?
- **The clock rule** (v3.1 "Also in this update", [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md)
  §8): time never goes back, moves forward only when two friends' messages show it is
  behind, and strangers can only trigger a warning. Can a friend, two colluding friends
  or a stranger push the clock somewhere harmful, or lock someone out?
- **"Also in this update":** the one-device-per-person decision, the move to a private
  signing key, removing `YapBluetooth.kt`, and the boot receiver with the battery
  optimisation request. Say if any of these weakens security or breaks something else
  in the update. The signing and boot items are not cryptography, so a short comment is
  enough.

### B. First reviews

Each proposal ends with its own "open questions for the reviewer". Please answer them
all, in this order, and add anything else you find.

**Encrypted pairing** ("Risks and open questions for the reviewer"):
1. Is `Noise_NNpsk0` with a QR-derived pre-shared key the right pattern, given that
   identities are proved afterwards by signed confirmations? Would `XXpsk3` with the
   identity keys be better, and is it worth the extra key handling?
2. Is binding the confirmations to the Noise handshake hash enough to tie them to this
   channel?
3. Which Rust Noise library, and at what version?
4. The pairing file (§4).
5. Whether the destination tokens used to find the pairing link leak anything worth
   hiding.

**Local storage** ("Open questions for the reviewer"):
1. redb with per-record XChaCha20-Poly1305 and HMAC keys, or SQLite with SQLCipher?
2. Is the metadata left visible on disk (record counts and sizes per unnamed chat)
   acceptable?
3. Encrypting the carrier store: worth the CPU?
4. The starting values in §5.
5. Applying group events one at a time (§6).
6. The clock rule (§8), especially how links report time in rule 3.

**Attachments** ("Open questions for the reviewer"):
1. STREAM over XChaCha20-Poly1305 for files: chunk size, and how the hash and chunk
   count are bound.
2. The direct-transfer key made from `linkKey` (§3).
3. Whether pieces of one attachment, sent together, can be linked by strangers through
   timing and size, and whether to space them out.
4. The carrier store byte limit (§4).
5. The starting values: 256 KB, 60 seconds, 100 MB, 5 GB.

Also check how the three fit together and with the rest of the update. For example: does
local storage hold everything pairing and the pair secret need, does attachment
transfer depend on `linkKey` and recognition in a way that survives "hiding from
friends" (v3.1 §3.9), and do the carrier store limits in the storage and attachment
proposals agree?

### D. Group encryption: batch container and newcomer's first piece

This was open in your second review (findings 7 and 13). It is now written, and our
answers mark both as fixed. Please check whether that is true.

Read [group-encryption-v1.md](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md), **§3.11 "Inbox keys
and the batch container"**, and check:
1. **Inbox keys.** Each member has an inbox key, published in their join request and
   replaced at each of their key changes. Is the key sound to publish that way? Does it
   leak anything to strangers, and can a stale or substituted key be used against a
   member?
2. **Piece labels** made from the recipient's inbox key and the sender's member key.
   Check the inputs, domain separation, and whether labels can be linked across pieces,
   batches or key changes.
3. **The `YGB1` batch layout and its rules.** A bounded scan of 128 labels; refusal of
   the whole batch for bad lengths; failure of one piece without losing the rest. Look
   for parsing, length and resource-use problems.
4. **Finding pieces.** How a newcomer, and a member who fell behind, find their pieces.
   Can either be locked out or sent the wrong piece?
5. **Acknowledgements.** The store and install acknowledgements are now separate. Is the
   split sound, and what happens if one arrives without the other?

## Questions beyond the open ones

These are the ones we think are most likely to hide a problem.

1. **Copy accounting.** Can the reservation table in v3.1 §1.4 be driven into a state
   where a copy is lost or counted twice, by a crash, a link closing mid-transfer or a
   hostile peer?
2. **Retiring `DELIVERED`.** With nobody confirming delivery, how long does a delivered
   message keep being offered, and is the traffic it causes still bounded (v3.1 §1.3,
   §1.8)?
3. **Pair secrets and groups.** After the weekly step, what happens to a phone that
   misses a week, a contact added mid-week, or a phone restored from a copy? Do labels,
   outer keys and recognition tags all agree?
4. **Encrypted pairing.** Is anything still sent in clear before the handshake
   completes, and does the pairing-link queue rule really stop pairing packets reaching
   a carrier or the internet relay?
5. **Local storage.** If power fails at any step, can a session move forward without its
   message being saved, or a message be shown twice? Is the wrapped store key handled so
   it fails closed?
6. **Attachments.** Can a stranger learn the size or type of an attachment from the
   pieces, and can a crafted file hurt the phone before the limits apply?
7. **Across everything.** Anything that would break the v2 rule, lose messages, or let
   one phone overload others.

## How to write the findings

Follow the format of
[the second review](2026-11-27-local-mesh-v3.1-second-review.md):
- reviewer, date and the revision you read at the top, then a one-paragraph verdict;
- for the third review, a table or list with one line for each of the second review's
  fifteen findings: answered, partly answered or not answered;
- findings numbered and graded **Critical, High, Medium or Low**, each with its
  locations, what is wrong, and a suggested fix;
- say where the proposals' claims about the code are right or wrong;
- say plainly what you could not check.

Ground rules:
- This is an **AI-assisted review, not a professional security audit.** Say so in the
  report. Do not describe it as an audit, and do not claim an independent expert has
  looked at this.
- Never invent cryptography. Where a fix needs a new construction, say what property
  it must have and name a standard one if there is one; do not make up a scheme.
- Do not mark anything as approved for building.
- Write the report to `docs/audit/2026-11-28-v3.1-update-review.md` and change no other
  file.
- Use plain British English.
