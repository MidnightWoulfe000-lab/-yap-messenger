## What to produce

### 1. Disposition of your fifth review's twelve findings

A table with one row per finding (1 to 12): **answered**, **partly answered** or **not
answered**, with the evidence in the current text, as your fifth review did for the
fourth. "Answered" should mean the written defect is answered, not that it is built.
Where we marked a finding "accepted, fixed", say whether you agree. Where we accepted a
cost (for example the lifetime catch-up keys in finding 2), say whether the claim about
it is now narrow and honest enough.

### 2. New findings

Grade each **Critical, High, Medium or Low**. For each give:
- the locations (file, section, line);
- what is wrong, with a **concrete failure case** (inputs, state, what goes wrong);
- a **suggested fix**. Never invent cryptography: where a fix needs a new construction,
  say what property it must have and name a standard one if there is one.

### 3. The new rules we most want checked

These have had no review at all, so look at them closely and trace them as an
implementer would:

- **Catch-up session selection and the limited first payload**: [group encryption](../proposals/group-encryption-v1.md)
  §3.2 and §3.11. Both identity orders, both phones behind, a lost set-up, a lost reply,
  repeated hints, and a restart at each installation step. Is the first payload limit
  strict enough to support the narrower historical-confidentiality claim?
- **The replay list cap and the failed set-up work order** (group §3.2, §3.11): can the
  lifetime used-base-key list be filled, or the failed-attempt work budget be used up, in
  a way that locks honest members out or lets replays through?
- **The sending generation for hiding**: [mesh v3.1](../proposals/local-mesh-v3.1-group-envelopes.md)
  §3.3 and §3.9. With a stalled table worker, a queued probe, an immediate reconnect,
  contact deletion and a week boundary, can a tag for a hidden friend still go out?
- **Pairing discovery and dispatch**: [encrypted pairing](../proposals/encrypted-pairing.md)
  §2 and §3. Resume after Ready or a restart, with and without a QR open, and the work
  an unsolicited frame can cause.
- **The pair-wide friend request with a signed expiry**: pairing §5. Crossed requests in
  different groups, group departure, delayed first delivery, late replay after the
  tombstone, and bootstrap retry.
- **Storage tiers, incremental reclamation and admission order**:
  [local storage](../proposals/local-storage-v1.md) §5, §7 and §7a. Do the reclamation
  calls, the physical floor and the admission order agree with each other? Can a receipt
  or key change still get through when ordinary rates and space are used up?
- **The batch work bound**: group §3.11. Total outer-layer work for one batch, repeated
  labels, batch retention when the phone has no piece, and the 33-candidate case.
- **The waiting line**: mesh v3.1 §1.4. Does it give an implementable service rule for
  whole 64-entry offers, and is the starvation claim now stated honestly?
- **Durable attachment ranges**: [attachments](../proposals/attachments-v1.md) §2 to §4,
  with storage §7a. Power loss after each chunk write and each range commit, failed final
  validation, and reservation accounting for finished files.
- **The clock additions**: storage §8. Serialised authority, startup order across the two
  stores, a recreated carrier store, and what a friend, two friends or a stranger can do.

Also check that the earlier tests and summaries no longer contradict the chosen
lifecycle (your finding 12), and that the proposals agree with each other: for example
the carrier store limits across storage and attachments, and whether attachment transfer
survives "hiding from friends".

### 4. Is the design ready to start building?

Say plainly **yes or no**. If no, list exactly what still blocks it, and separate:
- **blockers**: undefined states, transitions or failure behaviour that an implementer
  would have to invent;
- **items that can stay** as measured starting values (headroom, reserves, copy counts,
  rates) or as stated limits (restored-store rollback, copied devices, sponsor log
  completeness, sustained flooding).

Do not mark anything as approved for building unless you are prepared to say so in so
many words, and say what that does and does not mean. Independent expert review before
sensitive use is a separate matter, and still outstanding.

### 5. The direct-transfer channel

The direct-transfer channel and the `FILE_*` message and range encodings are **still
unwritten**. We left them for a later round. Tell us whether you consider that a blocker
for the whole update, or only for big attachments. Small attachments travel as pieces
through the ordinary carrier path (attachments §2). Say what, if anything, in the rest
of the update depends on the unwritten part.

### 6. Experiments

Rerun what you can and say what you could not. State which claims each experiment
supports and which it does not. We are not asking for new experiments, but if a finding
needs one, say which and what it must measure.

## How to write the report

Follow the format of [your fifth review](2026-11-28-v3.1-update-fifth-review.md):
- reviewer, date, the revision you read (HEAD `8953fcd`) and a one-paragraph verdict at
  the top;
- the disposition table, then the findings numbered and graded, each with locations, the
  failure case and a suggested fix;
- short assessments of the clock, recognition, attachment and size claims;
- the ready-to-build answer (section 4) and the direct-transfer answer (section 5);
- **verification limits**: say plainly what you checked by reading, by arithmetic and by
  running something, and what you did not (for example an Android build, interoperability
  tests, power-loss tests, fuzzing, radio or energy measurement);
- **SHA-256 fingerprints** of every input you read, as your fifth review did, so we can
  tell which text you saw.
